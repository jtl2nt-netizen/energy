# Hardware, integration and control stack for third-party dispatch of European small/mid flexible assets (status: October 2026)

Research notes for the report writer. Research date 11 Oct 2026. Every claim is dated and sourced inline; "historical" marks pre-2025 observations. Vendor marketing is labelled as such. Method note: the web-search budget for this session was exhausted after the first ~16 queries and several target domains (Enode, Viessmann developer portal, The Mobility House, E-REDES, photovoltaik.sh retry, Tesla developer) were blocked or returned 404, so the Gaps sections are long and honest. Three primary PDFs were downloaded and read in full text: the Neon/Naturstrom dynamic-tariff study (Oct 2025), the HTW Berlin Stromspeicher-Inspektion 2026, and the German TSOs' prequalification conditions (v1.05, 5 Jul 2024).

## KQ1. Residential batteries/inverters: which OEMs expose third-party charge/discharge control, through which interfaces, and what the German §14a / Smart-Meter-Gateway control path requires

### Takeaway
Local Modbus-TCP/SunSpec or local-API control of charge/discharge is available for most inverter/battery brands sold in Germany (SMA, Fronius GEN24, Kostal Plenticore, Huawei SUN2000, SolarEdge hybrid, Sungrow SH, GoodWe, Growatt, Solax G3/G4, Deye, Sigenergy, E3/DC, Fenecon, RCT, Victron, senec .Home, Tesla via cloud Fleet API), while Enphase, Varta, Solarwatt and the older sonnen eco/comfort lines are read-only in the open-source evcc device registry (2026). The regulated §14a path (SMGW + CLS Steuerbox) is a DSO curtailment path, not a market-dispatch path: boxes are certified but DSOs "hardly send commands" and most 2026 control is preventive time windows; the market participant's realistic dispatch path is the OEM cloud/local API or an integration layer (gridX, evcc-class software, OpenEMS/FEMS).

### Cited Findings

**Which brands are controllable (open-source registry evidence, 2026)**
- The evcc device registry (German page, read 11 Oct 2026) marks "Batteriesteuerung" (active battery control: discharge lock, grid charging, hold) as supported for: SMA Sunny Boy Storage 1.5–6.0 and Sunny Island (Modbus) and SMA Smart Energy hybrid; E3/DC; sonnenBatterie (but sonnen comfort, eco 5, eco 6, oem 6.5 are read-only); Fronius Primo/Symo GEN24 Plus and Verto Plus; Huawei SUN2000 hybrid and SmartLogger (Huawei EMMA read-only); SolarEdge hybrid (plain SolarEdge inverters only curtailable); Kostal Plenticore Hybrid (Piko Hybrid read-only); Solax hybrid X1/X3 G3/G4 (Solax cloud read-only); Growatt hybrid; Sungrow SH hybrid (iHomeManager read-only); Fenecon via Modbus API and REST API; senec .Home (.Connect cloud, .Home E4/P4 read-only); Tesla Powerwall via Fleet API (cloud); Sigenergy Sigen Hybrid / PV Max / SigenStor EC; Deye 3-phase hybrid; GoodWe ET/EH/BH/BT/ESA hybrids; Victron; RCT Power. Read-only: Enphase IQ Gateway, Varta element/pulse, Solarwatt MyReserve/EnergyManager. BYD did not appear in the visible list (BYD HVS/HVM packs are DC-coupled behind Fronius/SMA/Kostal inverters, so control goes through the inverter). — [evcc device list (DE)](https://docs.evcc.io/de/meters)
- evcc's battery-feature page states that "many newer battery/hybrid inverters offer an interface for active control"; without it the software can only react to SoC. Functions implemented on top of the interface: discharge lock, grid charging when price falls below a limit (with optional `maxsoc`), and a hold mode that pauses grid charging and blocks discharge during an active §14a reduction. — [evcc battery features](https://docs.evcc.io/de/features/battery)
- Community EMS templates control batteries via Modbus with a watchdog: SolarEdge battery setpoints behind a 60 s watchdog; GoodWe via register 47511 (EMSPowerMode) / 47512 (EMSPowerSet) with a 30 s timeout. These are community docs, not vendor guidance. — [evcc SunSpec/Modbus wiki](https://deepwiki.com/evcc-io/evcc/3.5.1-sunspec-and-modbus-meters)
- SMA's SunSpec Modbus technical information warns that "unauthorized alterations will void guarantee and warranty claims" (refers to device alterations; what counts as authorised third-party control is not defined there). — [SMA SunSpec Modbus TI](https://files.sma.de/downloads/STP60_SHP75_STPS60-SunSpec_Modbus-TI-en-15.pdf)
- HTW Berlin Stromspeicher-Inspektion 2026 (tested systems: SAX Power, Kostal ×2, SMA, Fronius, Fox ESS, RCT Power, Energy Depot) lists "price-optimised battery charging with grid power under dynamic tariffs and time-variable grid fees" as available for all eight systems, but for Kostal only "with several external EMS (e.g. Kiwigrid, Solar Manager, gridX)" and for Fox ESS only via the separate Consolinno Leaflet EMS. — [HTW Stromspeicher-Inspektion 2026, Table 2](https://solar.htw-berlin.de/wp-content/uploads/HTW-aquu-Stromspeicher-Inspektion-2026.pdf)
- Forum reports (historical, undated): E3/DC exposes live values over Modbus TCP, but control of the Hauskraftwerk is not intended over Modbus (vendor RSCP protocol needed for daily values). — [ElabNET forum](https://forum.timberwolf.io/viewtopic.php?t=2234)

**Warranty terms relevant to third-party cycling (2026)**
- HTW 2026 warranty table (German warranty documents): product warranty 5–10 years; performance warranty 10–12 years; end-of-warranty throughput caps: BYD 2.4 MWh per kWh, SMA 7.2 MWh/kWh (60 % residual), Fronius 3.1 MWh/kWh or 3,120 full cycles, Fox ESS 2.9 MWh/kWh, RCT 3.5 MWh/kWh or 3,500 cycles, Energy Depot 6,000 cycles, SAX 10,000 cycles incl. partial cycles. HTW notes warranty documents range from 3 to 18 pages and differ by country. — [HTW 2026, Table 2 and ch. 4](https://solar.htw-berlin.de/wp-content/uploads/HTW-aquu-Stromspeicher-Inspektion-2026.pdf)
- Dutch guidance for imbalance-traded home batteries: imbalance trading means ~2 full cycles/day vs ~1 with solar alone; at 6,000 cycles that is ~8 years vs ~16 years; consumers are told to check whether the warranty permits intensive trading. — [thuisbatterijnederland.nl, 5 Aug 2026](https://thuisbatterijnederland.nl/onbalansmarkt-thuisbatterij/)

**Round-trip efficiency reality (independent measurement, 2026)**
- HTW measured system efficiency for grid-charge arbitrage: a very efficient system (Fronius Symo GEN24 10.0 Plus + Reserva 12.6) reaches 78 % round-trip when charged at 4 kW and discharged at 200 W; an inefficient device loses 37 %. Discharge efficiency falls below 95 % under 500 W and below 90 % under 300 W; at 100 W some systems return <50 %. Batteries are typically grid-charged at 2–6 kW. At 60 % system efficiency the charge/discharge spread must be ≥14 ct/kWh; at 70 % efficiency, charging at 28 ct requires discharging above 40 ct. — [HTW 2026, pp. 39–44](https://solar.htw-berlin.de/wp-content/uploads/HTW-aquu-Stromspeicher-Inspektion-2026.pdf)
- HTW: grid charging of PV-coupled batteries is permitted, requires an intelligent metering system (iMSys), and stored grid power "currently may not be fed back into the grid" / no EEG remuneration can be claimed for it (as of the 2026 edition, pre-MiSpeL). — [HTW 2026, p. 40](https://solar.htw-berlin.de/wp-content/uploads/HTW-aquu-Stromspeicher-Inspektion-2026.pdf)

**§14a / SMGW / CLS / Steuerbox control path (2025–2026)**
- Mid-2025 about eight BSI-certified Steuerboxen were on the market; on 18 Jun 2025 eBZ, EFR, Robotron and Power Plus Communications were certified; Swistec's SwiSBox (Sept 2024) was the first gateway-integrated box; gateway-integrated boxes not expected broadly before summer 2026. "First boxes installed, but not yet used"; DSOs send "kaum Befehle" (hardly any commands); most control runs as preventive time windows of up to two hours per day. Devices are addressed via EEBus, Modbus or IEC 61850; the EMS variant has the DSO set a total cap at the connection point with a simultaneity factor of ~0.8–0.45. — [Innobu, Stand 12 Jun 2026](https://www.innobu.com/de/artikel/steuerbox-14a-netzsteuerung-2026.html)
- CLS is a transport path, not an application protocol; "many metering operators have not yet enabled CLS channels"; BSI certification for control boxes is costly, few devices exist; DSOs often use IP-VPN instead. EEBus LPC (limit power consumption) sets a watt ceiling with validity period, failsafe value and heartbeat; LPP is the feed-in counterpart; SG-Ready is a switching command only. Wallboxes: OCPP 1.6J/2.0.1 to backend, ISO 15118-20 to vehicle, setpoints via SetChargingProfile; "an aggregator uses the manufacturer's backend" as the alternative to CLS. Claim (unverified at model level): Vaillant, Viessmann, Stiebel Eltron, Bosch and Wolf heat pumps produced from 2025 ship with EEBus. — [stromfee.me, 2026](https://www.stromfee.me/post/14a-schnittstellen-im-hausanschluss-welche-standards-installateure-2026-kennen-sollten)
- SMA's §14a page: remote control only once an iMSys is installed, with a Steuerbox (CLS adapter) added; two variants, direct device control or via Sunny Home Manager 2.0 using EEBus use case LPC; grandfathering for pre-2024 devices until 31 Dec 2028; preventive control allowed for at most 24 months from first use; time-variable grid fees since April 2025. No mention of Modbus or third-party control on that page. — [SMA §14a](https://www.sma.de/energiewirtschaftsgesetz2025)
- Costs: SMGW and CLS-Steuerbox each capped at 50 €/yr (100 €/yr combined); Module 1 = 80 € fixed + 20 % off grid-fee work price on a flat 3,750 kWh, typically 120–200 €/yr; Module 2 = 60 % off work price with separate meter (~25 €/yr) and usually second supply contract, 200–500+ €/yr; Module 3 = time-variable fees supplementing Module 1, high-load up to 200 % of standard, low-load 50–80 % cheaper, mandatory offer since 1 Apr 2025, adds 50–150 €/yr, requires iMSys; BSI TR-03109-6 v2.0 expected binding ~2027. — [elektronik-zeit.de, updated 4 Oct 2026](https://elektronik-zeit.de/14a-enwg/)
- §14a: devices >4.2 kW commissioned from 1 Jan 2024 must be controllable; DSO may reduce to 4.2 kW but not switch off; wallboxes >11 kW need DSO approval within two months; typical relief 110–190 €/yr. — [Moddemann, 16 Mar 2026](https://www.moddemann.de/artikel/bidirektionales-laden-2026/)
- Smart-meter rollout "well above 20 %" in 2026 (no exact figure given). — [stromfee.me](https://www.stromfee.me/post/14a-schnittstellen-im-hausanschluss-welche-standards-installateure-2026-kennen-sollten)

**Integration-layer vendors (2026)**
- gridX (XENON): "55+ OEMs and 500+ devices", 250k+ connected assets in 12 countries, 13k active flex systems, ">40 MWh of flexibility traded daily", customers E.ON, Essent, IBC Solar (HEMS) and Fastned, Allego, CUBOS (EVCI); gridBox local gateway plus cloud connectivity and "unified API"; no public pricing ("Get in touch"). — [gridx.ai](https://www.gridx.ai/)
- Enode: no published enterprise per-device price; historical (~2022) Starter plan allowed 5 devices with unlimited calls; "low fixed cost per device" per an investor profile; custom plans on request. — [Enode blog (historical)](https://enode.com/blog/product-updates/enode-is-now-open-to-everyone); [Lowercarbon profile](https://lowercarbon.com/company/enode/)
- Tesla Fleet API (used for Powerwall/vehicle control): pay-per-use, illustrative "$1.21 for 1,211 commands" (~$0.001/command), $10/month credit for small apps, rate limits 60 realtime-data req/min, 3 wakes/min, 30 commands/min per device; paid access enforced since Feb 2024. — [Tesla Fleet API billing](https://developer.tesla.com/docs/fleet-api/billing-and-limits)
- OpenEMS: open source; Fenecon FEMS is built on it; Modbus master; EEBus integration via Fraunhofer ISE's jEEBus library, with Fenecon planning EEBus on FEMS in mid-2026; OCPP and embedded-UI modes are hackathon topics, not shipped features. — [openems.io](https://openems.io/)
- Viessmann developer portal: Basic plan free; other API packages and enterprise terms not public. — [Viessmann developer portal](https://developer.viessmann-climatesolutions.com/)

### Inferences
- For a software-only vendor, residential battery dispatch does not need proprietary hardware: a cloud-to-local path exists for the majority of 2020+ hybrid inverters via Modbus TCP/local API, usually behind a HEMS/gateway (evcc-class, gridX gridBox, FEMS) that handles the watchdog/failsafe. The commercially robust path is partnering with an existing HEMS/integration layer rather than building Modbus drivers per OEM.
- Warranty risk is quantifiable: throughput caps (2.4–7.2 MWh/kWh) and cycle caps (3,120–10,000) mean that two cycles/day (730/yr) exhausts a 3,120-cycle Fronius warranty in ~4.3 years and a BYD 2.4 MWh/kWh cap in ~3.3 years at full-DoD; arbitrage schedules must be cycle-aware.
- The SMGW/CLS Steuerbox is irrelevant as a dispatch channel in 2026 (certified but mostly unused, DSO-only semantics). Its relevance for a software vendor is (a) Module 3 price signals that an optimiser must read, and (b) the obligation to honour LPC caps (hold mode).
- HTW's efficiency findings imply that most residential arbitrage value in Germany is destroyed at low discharge power; an optimiser's edge is in discharge scheduling against household load, not just price spreads.

### Gaps
- No vendor warranty document found that explicitly addresses third-party (non-OEM-app) cycling; HTW's table gives caps but not the legal treatment of external control. sonnen, E3/DC, senec, Huawei, SolarEdge, Enphase, Sigenergy, Deye warranty texts not retrieved.
- Enode's current supported-brand list and pricing: the vendors page redirected to a login page; not retrieved. gridX, KIWIGRID and SMGW provider price lists not public.
- No source on whether any OEM VPP (sonnenVPP, senec.Cloud, E3/DC, 1KOMMA5° Heartbeat, Sigenergy) contractually locks dispatch; this could not be searched after the budget ran out.
- Iberia-specific inverter mix and E-REDES/Mobi.E rules: no search could be run; both E-REDES URLs tried returned 404.

## KQ2. EV charging: commercial reality of bidirectional charging in 2026 vs smart unidirectional (V1G)

### Takeaway
Commercial V2G in Germany exists since Feb 2026 as closed OEM–utility bundles (BMW iX3 Neue Klasse + E.ON, 11 kW bidirectional BMW wallbox, 0.24 €/h plug-in bonus capped at 720 €/yr plus 0.40 €/kWh for exported energy; VW/Elli announced the same 720 €/yr cap for a Q4 2026 launch), after the Nov 2025 EnWG reform ended double grid fees and electricity tax on EV feed-back and the BNetzA MiSpeL process (from April 2026) allowed operation without a second meter. The dispatch rights in every live V2G offer sit with the OEM/utility pair (BMW/E.ON, VW/Elli, Renault-Mobilize/The Mobility House, Octopus/BYD-Zaptec in the UK); no open API for third-party V2G dispatch was found. V1G via OEM telematics or OCPP is open and worth ~164 €/yr per car on a German dynamic tariff (Neon, Oct 2025), rising to ~440 €/yr once §14a Module 1 + 3 grid-fee relief is stacked.

### Cited Findings

**Regulation (Germany, Nov 2025 – 2026)**
- Bundestag passed the EnWG amendment on 13 Nov 2025: electricity fed back from EV batteries is treated like stationary storage, no longer double-charged grid fees from 2026; MiSpeL (BNetzA) simplification planned from April 2026, allowing V2G without a second meter; DSOs need 6–12 months to implement. — [firmenauto, 14 Nov 2025](https://www.firmenauto.de/branche/v2g-bidirektionales-laden-enwg-reform-2026-netzentgelte-fuer-flotten/)
- E3/DC: the exemption covers grid fees and electricity tax on energy fed back by stationary storage and bidirectional EVs; Bundesrat approval still pending at the time; applicable to end consumers from February 2026 (other sources say 1 Jan 2026); MiSpeL separates EEG-eligible feed-in from exemption-eligible feed-in and includes a flat-rate model for PV+storage households. Marcus Fendt (The Mobility House) estimates up to €700/yr per customer in Germany, "equal to the charging cost of 15,000–20,000 km". — [E3/DC, Nov 2025](https://www.e3dc.com/v2g-netzentgeltbefreiung-fuer-bidirektionales-laden-kommt/)
- MiSpeL final details still "in progress" in March 2026; EEG does not automatically cover power discharged from a vehicle; PV small-business tax simplifications do not clearly extend to V2G; Kleinunternehmer thresholds 25,000 € (prior year) / 100,000 € (current). — [Moddemann, 16 Mar 2026](https://www.moddemann.de/artikel/bidirektionales-laden-2026/)
- Search-result summary (secondary blogs, Dec 2025–2026): some sources expect MiSpeL metering rules to be finalised only in early Oct 2026 and broad V2G ramp-up from 2028; technical standardisation runs to end-2027. Treat as unverified. — [schmidtisblog](https://www.schmidtisblog.de/bidirektionales-laden-v2g-2026-enwg-fallstricke-1817487/); [basic-solar](https://basic-solar.de/ratgeber/bidirektionales-laden-was-wird-wichtig-in-2026/)

**Live commercial V2G products**
- BMW + E.ON (Germany): announced IAA Sept 2025, orderable from Feb 2026; BMW iX3 (Neue Klasse, Gen-6 eDrive) first; BMW Wallbox Professional 11 kW bidirectional; E.ON "ÖkoStrom Home & Drive V2G" tariff with "V2G Plus" feed-in contract and a smart meter if not present; plug-in bonus 0.24 €/h with V2G activated, capped at 60 €/month / 720 €/yr (≈250 h/month plugged in), paid even if no energy is transferred; 0.40 €/kWh for energy fed to the grid; only full-feed-in PV systems supported, not hybrid PV setups; first 100 customers get 700 € off the wallbox. — [IAA Mobility, 13 Feb 2026](https://www.iaa-mobility.com/en/newsroom/news/future-technology/bmw-eon-officially-launch-bidirectional-v2g-offering-germany); [BMWBLOG, 9 Feb 2026](https://www.bmwblog.com/2026/02/09/bmw-neue-klasse-v2g-bidirectional-charging-eon-partnership/)
- VW + Elli (Germany): "to launch V2G offering in Q4 2026" (17 Apr 2026) and "launch V2G offer with up to €720 annual bonus" (23 Jun 2026); Elli piloted a bidirectional home charger developed with Cubos (9 Sep 2025). Headlines only; eligible ID. models, wallbox and tariff details not retrieved. — [electrive search listing](https://www.electrive.com/?s=bidirectional+Elli)
- Renault/Mobilize + The Mobility House (France, historical Oct 2024): Renault 5 and Alpine A290, PowerBox Verso AC bidirectional wallbox, "Mobilize Power" contract, "free charging and driving" marketing; relies on France having no double grid fees; UK 2025; "Germany still developing its approach". — [pv magazine press release, 22 Oct 2024](https://www.pv-magazine.de/unternehmensmeldungen/kostenlos-laden-renault-group-mobilize-und-the-mobility-house-starten-vehicle-to-grid-in-frankreich/); [The Mobility House newsroom](https://www.mobilityhouse.com/int_en/our-company/newsroom/article/mobilize-selects-the-mobility-house-for-their-vehicle-to-grid-service). Renault states battery warranty is unaffected by V2G use (vendor claim). — [stromfahrer.de (historical)](https://www.stromfahrer.de/v2g-news)
- Octopus Power Pack (UK, live 2026): only BYD Dolphin + Zaptec Pro qualify; free charging if plugged in 12 h/day on 20 days/month and under 210 kWh/month (~625 miles); minimum SoC below 30 % required; exported EV energy earns no separate payment; smart meter and G99 certificate required; Octopus does not yet install bidirectional chargers. — [Octopus Power Pack](https://octopus.energy/power-pack/)
- Octopus Germany: EV leasing bundle with tariffs and home chargers announced for early 2026; no evidence it includes V2G. — [EV Infrastructure News](https://www.evinfrastructurenews.com/ev-technology/octopus-energy-v2g-german-ev)
- DC bidirectional hardware: Sigenergy EVDC charging module, 12.5 or 25 kW, 150–1000 V, CCS2, part of SigenStor; V2H already works for several Hyundai/Kia models (dealer article). — [Moddemann, Mar 2026](https://www.moddemann.de/artikel/bidirektionales-laden-2026/)
- Wallbox cost ranges quoted in press: bidirectional wallboxes 3,000–8,000 € (secondary). — [eleport V2G guide](https://eleport.com/v2g-vehicle-to-grid/)

**V1G value per car**
- Neon/Naturstrom (hourly simulation Sept 2024–Aug 2025, Westnetz grid area, VW ID.3 45 kWh, 10,442 km/yr, 2/3 charged at home, ≥60 % SoC at 06:00, 75 % of time at home): dynamic tariff with immediate charging saves ~nothing (537 → 530 €/yr); smart charging saves 164 €/yr (→373 €, −31 %); with §14a Module 1 (169 €/yr Westnetz discount for 4.2 kW limitation) plus Module 3 time-variable fees, home charging cost falls to 93–94 €/yr (−82 %). Bidirectional charging not modelled. — [Neon, 27 Oct 2025](https://neon.energy/Neon-Dynamischer-Stromtarif.pdf)
- Netherlands (vendor claim): average Gridio user cut home-charging cost ~25 % through timing; 2025 cheapest-vs-most-expensive-hour spread widened to €92/MWh. — [Gridio blog](https://www.gridio.io/blog/2025-electricity-prices-5-insights-for-dutch-ev-drivers)
- Peer-reviewed modelling (German 2030 households): smart charging without PV "only returns small savings"; combined HEMS with PV+HP+EV 900–1,200 €/yr. — [Applied Energy, 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S030626192402381X)

**Control interfaces for V1G**
- Two routes for wallboxes: grid-operator control via CLS/IP-VPN, or an aggregator using the manufacturer's backend; OCPP 1.6J or 2.0.1 to backend, SetChargingProfile for setpoints, ISO 15118-20 to vehicle; local EMS can conflict with external setpoints. — [stromfee.me](https://www.stromfee.me/post/14a-schnittstellen-im-hausanschluss-welche-standards-installateure-2026-kennen-sollten)
- Tesla vehicle/Powerwall control via Fleet API is metered per command (see KQ1). — [Tesla](https://developer.tesla.com/docs/fleet-api/billing-and-limits)
- SMA EV Charger / eCharger / EV Charger Business support §14a-conformant direct control. — [SMA](https://www.sma.de/energiewirtschaftsgesetz2025)

### Inferences
- Every live European V2G product is a vertically integrated bundle: OEM-specified wallbox + OEM-chosen utility/aggregator + OEM app. A third-party optimiser can only enter V2G as a subcontracted dispatcher to the OEM/utility (as The Mobility House is to Renault), not by connecting to the car directly.
- The BMW/E.ON economics are a plug-in availability payment (0.24 €/h) rather than energy arbitrage; the 720 €/yr cap is paid mostly for availability, so the marginal value of better optimisation software inside such a bundle is small; the value sits in the aggregation contract.
- V1G is the repeatable, legal software wedge: control via OCPP (own backend or partner CPO) or OEM telematics (Enode/own integrations), value ~160 €/yr gross on a German dynamic tariff per Neon, ~440 €/yr when Module 1+3 are stacked, with no hardware beyond a smart meter for the tariff.
- "Double grid fee" removal is law; the practical blocker for V2G in Oct 2026 is metering/MiSpeL process implementation at DSOs (6–12 months) and the OEM-locked hardware.

### Gaps
- No retrievable 2026 German Mobilize/The Mobility House tariff terms (the TMH German V2G page 404'd); no numbers on actual realised V2G revenue per car from any provider (only caps and marketing "up to").
- ISO 15118-20 vehicle list (Hyundai/Kia, Polestar, Cupra, Ford, Mercedes, Nissan AC vs DC), OCPP 2.1 adoption, VDE-AR-N 4105 metering specifics for V2G, and Kostal/Ambibox/sun2wheel/Wallbox Quasar 2 prices: search budget exhausted before these could be queried.
- V1G backend vendors (ev.energy, Jedlix, Monta, Tibber, 1KOMMA5°, Octopus Intelligent Go DE) and Dutch imbalance / Nordic FCR per-car revenue: no sources retrieved; the Dutch EV imbalance value per car was explicitly not found in results.

## KQ3. Heat pumps and hot water: SG-Ready, EEBUS, vendor APIs, §14a dimming, value of time-shifting

### Takeaway
Heat-pump flexibility in Germany is worth roughly 120 €/yr per heat pump from the dynamic tariff alone (buffer-only flexibility, Neon 2025) but ~480–525 €/yr when §14a Module 1+3 or Module 2 grid-fee relief is stacked, i.e. the regulated grid-fee relief is 3–4× the market value. Interfaces: SG-Ready contacts (switching only) remain the installed base; EEBus LPC is the de-facto new standard for 2025+ units; vendor cloud APIs exist (Viessmann has a free basic developer tier) but commercial/aggregator terms were not retrievable.

### Cited Findings
- Neon/Naturstrom (17,500 kWh_th house, 3.7 kW_el air-water HP, 8 kW heating rod, 15 kWh_th buffer, SCOP 3, only buffer flexibility): dynamic tariff without shifting ≈ fixed price (1,873 → 1,868 €/yr); with smart heating −7 % (1,749 €), saving 124 €/yr; with Module 1+3: 1,390 € (−26 %, 482 €/yr saved); with Module 2: 1,348 € (−28 %, 525 €/yr). Authors say savings "can be considerably higher" if building thermal inertia or small room-temperature deviations are allowed. — [Neon, 27 Oct 2025](https://neon.energy/Neon-Dynamischer-Stromtarif.pdf); summarised by [pv magazine, 27 Oct 2025](https://www.pv-magazine.de/2025/10/27/studie-sieht-hohes-einsparpotenzial-durch-dynamische-stromtarife-und-zeitvariable-netzentgelte/)
- Module 3 adds ~56 €/yr over Module 1 for a heat pump (150 → ~200 €/yr) in a separate consumer-site calculation. — [energie-experten.org](https://www.energie-experten.org/news/modul-1-2-oder-3-wann-lohnt-sich-welches-waermepumpen-modul)
- Vendor/comparison sites: ~124 €/yr for SG-Ready + HEMS smart HP, "400–800 €/yr with Module 1 and 3" (reduco, vendor); 1,000 kWh shifted × 8 ct = 80 €/yr (Solarwatt example); 100–200 €/yr extra for 4,000–5,000 kWh HPs (strompreisheute). — [reduco](https://reduco.ai/blog/heizung/dynamische-stromtarife-waermepumpe); [Solarwatt](https://www.solarwatt.de/ratgeber/dynamische-stromtarife); [strompreisheute](https://strompreisheute.de/wann-lohnt-sich-dynamischer-stromtarif)
- E.ON/FfE modelling: flexible households could save up to 1.65 bn €/yr in total (not HP-specific). — [heise, 2025](https://www.heise.de/news/Studie-Flexible-Stromnutzung-koennte-Haushalte-1-65-Milliarden-Euro-sparen-11451583.html)
- Interfaces: SG-Ready (since 2013) = two digital inputs / four states, state 1 corresponds to §14a dimming, switching only, no graded setpoint; EEBus LPC/LPP with failsafe and heartbeat is "de facto standard for new heat pumps"; claim that Vaillant, Viessmann, Stiebel Eltron, Bosch, Wolf ship EEBus from 2025; Bundesverband Wärmepumpe moving SG Ready 2.0 to EEBus use cases. — [stromfee.me, 2026](https://www.stromfee.me/post/14a-schnittstellen-im-hausanschluss-welche-standards-installateure-2026-kennen-sollten)
- Viessmann pilot with DSOs running in 2026 "without regular operation" (§14a path). — [Innobu](https://www.innobu.com/de/artikel/steuerbox-14a-netzsteuerung-2026.html)
- Viessmann developer API: Basic plan free; enterprise "contact us"; capabilities/aggregator terms not stated on landing page. — [Viessmann developer portal](https://developer.viessmann-climatesolutions.com/)
- OpenEMS/Fenecon: EEBus (jEEBus) rollout on FEMS mid-2026. — [openems.io](https://openems.io/)

### Inferences
- A software vendor can reach heat pumps three ways: (1) through a HEMS partner that already speaks EEBus LPC/SG-Ready locally (gridX, FEMS, evcc-class), (2) through vendor cloud APIs where commercial use is permitted (Viessmann is the only one with a public developer portal found), (3) through the utility's §14a/Module-3 tariff design. Only (1) and (2) deliver market dispatch; the §14a path is DSO-only.
- Because 75–80 % of the per-HP value comes from grid-fee relief rather than price arbitrage, the economically dominant "control" is the customer's tariff enrolment (Module 1/2/3), not the optimiser; an optimiser's incremental value over a dumb Module-3 schedule is likely well under 100 €/yr unless building inertia is exploited.

### Gaps
- No primary documentation retrieved for Vaillant (myVAILLANT), Stiebel Eltron ISG, Bosch, Daikin, NIBE, Lambda APIs or their third-party/aggregator terms; the EEBus-from-2025 claim is from an installer blog, not vendor datasheets.
- Fraunhofer ISE/IEE, Agora, HTW, FfE heat-pump-specific flexibility studies 2024–2026 could not be fetched (budget); only the Neon study and secondary summaries are cited.

## KQ4. C&I batteries and loads: control stack, trading interface, prequalification, shadow-pilot vs direct control

### Takeaway
German C&I/utility battery revenue is falling and converging: ISEA/RWTH cross-market index for a 1 MW/2 MWh system dropped from ~309 k€/MW/yr (2024) to ~259 k€ (2025), with FCR collapsing (>90 % lower realised contribution; 1.35 GW prequalified batteries vs 0.53–0.56 GW demand) and aFRR capacity becoming the anchor; the ISEA March 2026 annualised figure is ~200 k€/MW/yr and Environ's 12-month average ~155 k€/MW/yr (Aug 2026). The control stack to monetise this is dictated by TSO prequalification: 1–4 s real-time telemetry from the pool to the TSO, redundant links, control-system test, 5-year validity, free TSO-side application but provider-borne IT costs; in practice a direct marketer/aggregator owns the TSO interface and pooling, so a software vendor's entry point is the aggregator's or EMS vendor's API, not the TSO.

### Cited Findings

**Revenue benchmarks (Germany)**
- ISEA (RWTH) Battery Revenue Index, published 23 Jan 2026: cross-market (FCR+aFRR+intraday, max 2 cycles/day) 2-h system 309 k€ → 259 k€/MW/yr (−16 %); 1-h 183 k€ → 151 k€ (−17 %). Within the 2-h cross-market: FCR realised −90 %+, aFRR capacity +40 %, aFRR energy −65 %, intraday −16 %. Single markets: FCR 114 k€ → 106 k€/MW/yr; aFRR capacity 2-h >146 k€ (+39 %), 1-h >73 k€; aFRR energy 2-h 227 k€ → 76.5 k€; day-ahead 2-h 77.5 k€ → 91 k€, 1-h 38.7 k€ → 47 k€ (15-min products credited). Prequalified battery FCR 0.81 GW (early 2024) → 1.35 GW (early 2026) vs demand ~0.53–0.56 GW; aFRR 0.33 → 1.2 GW per direction vs ~1.9 GW demand. — [pv magazine DE, 23 Jan 2026](https://www.pv-magazine.de/2026/01/23/erloespotenziale-fuer-stationaere-batteriespeicher-in-deutschland-sind-2025-zurueckgegangen/)
- Aug 2026 summary: ISEA March 2026 annualised ~200 k€/MW/yr (1 MW/2 MWh); ISEA multi-use benchmark 2025/26 195–214 k€/MW/yr; Environ 12-month average ~155 k€/MW/yr (~12.9 k€/MW/month), forecast ~151.5 k€; monthly high 18.9 k€/MW (Sep 2025), low 5.7 k€/MW (Dec 2025), July 2026 16.95 k€/MW; FCR demand 550–600 MW vs ~1.35 GW prequalified; aFRR ~2 GW/direction vs ~1.2 GW prequalified. — [photovoltaik.sh, 30 Aug 2026](https://www.photovoltaik.sh/news/erloespotenziale-und-wirtschaftlichkeit-von-netzgekoppelten-grossbatteriespeichern-in-deutschland-2026)
- Clean Horizon via ESS News, 2 Sep 2026: German July 2026 revenues fell 10–16 % across configurations; upward aFRR capacity price fell from €15 to €10/MW/h; 1-h spread −23 %. Comparators July 2026 (€/MW/yr): Spain 477 k (2-h) / 650 k (4-h, +90 % m/m, day-ahead + intraday + mFRR spreads); Poland 581.8 k (2-h); France 410 k; Italy SUD 450 k; Italy NORD 165 k (4-h); Sweden 126 k (2-h, −20 %, FCR/mFRR capacity prices −30 %). — [ESS News, 2 Sep 2026](https://www.ess-news.com/2026/09/02/battery-revenues-surge-in-spain-and-poland-as-europe-sees-mixed-results/)
- Modo Energy (Nov 2025): new German inertia (Momentanreserve) remuneration, premium product with ≥90 % availability; batteries can add up to 20 k€/MW/yr. — [Modo Energy](https://modoenergy.com/research/de/inertia-germany-market-prices-grid-forming-inverters-grid-frequency-stability-november-2025)
- Co-location backtest (EERA, Oct 2025 data, published 1 Dec 2025): 1 MW/2 MWh with 3 MW PV, charging only from own PV; storage incremental gain ~28 k€/month under cross-market incl. positive aFRR (up to ~36 k€ at marginal prices); ~80 % of storage revenue from positive aFRR, rest intraday. — [EERA Battery Beats](https://www.eera-consulting.de/battery-beats/erloese-co-location-bess-2025-oktober)

**Prequalification and telemetry (TSO rules, v1.05 of 5 Jul 2024, still referenced 2026)**
- TSO-side PQ costs are not passed on ("Der PQ-Antrag ist in diesem Sinne kostenfrei"); provider bears all own costs incl. IT/communication requirements; PQ valid five years; every RE/RG must be part of a pool (a pool may consist of a single RE); real-time data at pool level 1–4 s for FCR/aFRR (in agreement with the connecting TSO), 1 min for mFRR; forced update cycle provider control system → TSO max 30 s, TE → provider max 60 s (recommended); test time series ≥4 h without gaps; full activation within 30 s (FCR), 5 min (aFRR), 12.5 min (mFRR); FCR/aFRR test runs evaluated on 1–4 s data; ≥95 % of actual values must be within tolerance band; limited-energy-reservoir rules apply when units cannot sustain power for 2 h (FCR) / 4 h (FRR). — [regelleistung.net PQ conditions PDF](https://www.regelleistung.net/xspproxy/api/StaticFiles/Regelleistung/Infos_f%C3%BCr_Anbieter/Wie_werde_ich_Regelenergieanbieter_Pr%C3%A4qualifikation/Pr%C3%A4qualifikationsbedingungen_FCR_aFRR_mFRR/PQ-Bedingungen-Delta_2024_07_05.pdf)
- Process: IT concept, deliverance concept, operating test with log, control-system test with the connecting TSO (correct data points, setpoint execution, redundant data links), grid-operator confirmation, submission via PQ portal; separate guides exist for pool concepts and battery redundancy requirements. — [regelleistung.net prequalification page](https://www.regelleistung.net/en-us/Become-a-balancing-service-provider/Prequalification)
- Secondary/vendor claims (unverified): PQ takes 4–8 weeks and 15–50 k€; or "several weeks to months"; needs real-time link to TSO control room, balancing-group contract and collateral; aggregator/direct marketer typically takes over PQ, bidding and settlement; the 1 MW minimum is reached via pooling. — [sunshineenergy.de](https://sunshineenergy.de/photovoltaik-lexikon/regelenergie-photovoltaik/); [stromfee.me](https://www.stromfee.me/post/regelenergiemarkt-batteriespeicher-2026)
- Historical: BNetzA 2019 decision on FCR conditions for storage. — [BK6-17-234](https://www.clearingstelle-eeg-kwkg.de/sites/default/files/BK6-17-234_beschluss_190502_PRL_Speicher.pdf)

**EMS / OpenEMS**
- OpenEMS is the open-source base of Fenecon FEMS; Modbus master; EEBus via jEEBus; OCPP integration and embedded UI are hackathon topics, not shipped features. — [openems.io](https://openems.io/)

### Inferences
- A read-only shadow pilot for a C&I battery needs only Modbus-TCP/SunSpec or EMS-API read access (SoC, power, meter) plus public market data; it can be delivered without any TSO interaction. Direct control for balancing markets requires the asset to sit in a prequalified pool with 1–4 s telemetry and redundant links to the TSO — a capability only direct marketers/aggregators (and large EMS vendors) hold. The vendor's dispatch right is therefore contractual with the direct marketer, not technical with the asset.
- With FCR saturated (2.4× demand) and aFRR capacity the main pillar, optimiser quality matters most in aFRR bidding and intraday; the measurable spread between optimisers (ISEA 195–214 k€ benchmark vs Environ ~155 k€ realised) is the sales argument for a software-only entrant, but that must be proven in a shadow pilot against the incumbent's realised P&L.
- Co-located batteries restricted to charging from own PV (EEG privilege) lose FCR and negative aFRR, which narrows the optimiser's problem and raises the relative importance of positive aFRR (80 % of storage revenue in the EERA case).

### Gaps
- No sources retrieved on C&I EMS vendors' trading interfaces (Tesvolt, Intilion, Commeo, ADS-TEC, Kyon, Eco Stor) or on direct-marketer fee structures (fixed vs % share, floors); photovoltaik.sh had none. Modo/Flexpower/enspired/Entrix fee data not found.
- Verified PQ cost/duration figures: only vendor blogs; TSO documents give no duration.
- German §14a Module-3-style value for C&I loads (non-battery) not researched.

## KQ7. Judgment: cheapest, most legal, most repeatable control path for a software-only vendor

### Takeaway
Ranked by (control availability × legal clarity × repeatability × value per integration hour): (1) C&I/utility batteries via an existing direct marketer or EMS vendor (read-only shadow pilot first, then optimiser-as-a-service; value 150–260 k€/MW/yr makes a single site worth more than ~1,000 home batteries); (2) V1G EV charging via OCPP/OEM telematics through a utility or CPO partner (open interfaces, ~160–440 €/car-yr, no hardware); (3) residential batteries via a HEMS/integration partner (gridX/FEMS/evcc-class) on Modbus/local APIs, where value is small in Germany but meaningful in NL; (4) heat pumps (value dominated by tariff enrolment, interface fragmentation); (5) V2G, which is OEM-locked hardware and bundles in 2026 and should be approached only as a subcontracted dispatcher to an OEM/utility. No proprietary hardware is required for (1)–(3); (4) needs an EEBus/SG-Ready-capable gateway from a partner; (5) needs OEM-specified bidirectional wallboxes.

### Cited Findings
- Software-only control exists today through: evcc-class local control over Modbus/local APIs for most hybrid inverters ([evcc](https://docs.evcc.io/de/meters)); gridX's 55+ OEM / 500+ device layer with 250k assets ([gridX](https://www.gridx.ai/)); OCPP SetChargingProfile and OEM backends for wallboxes ([stromfee](https://www.stromfee.me/post/14a-schnittstellen-im-hausanschluss-welche-standards-installateure-2026-kennen-sollten)); direct marketers/aggregators holding TSO prequalification and pooling ([stromfee](https://www.stromfee.me/post/regelenergiemarkt-batteriespeicher-2026); [regelleistung.net](https://www.regelleistung.net/en-us/Become-a-balancing-service-provider/Prequalification)).
- Paths that need hardware or are closed: V2G bundles (BMW wallbox + E.ON; Mobilize PowerBox + TMH; BYD + Zaptec + Octopus) ([IAA](https://www.iaa-mobility.com/en/newsroom/news/future-technology/bmw-eon-officially-launch-bidirectional-v2g-offering-germany); [pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.de/unternehmensmeldungen/kostenlos-laden-renault-group-mobilize-und-the-mobility-house-starten-vehicle-to-grid-in-frankreich/); [Octopus](https://octopus.energy/power-pack/)); §14a CLS Steuerbox path (certified, DSO-only, barely used) ([Innobu](https://www.innobu.com/de/artikel/steuerbox-14a-netzsteuerung-2026.html)); Kostal and Fox ESS price-optimised grid charging only via third-party EMS ([HTW 2026](https://solar.htw-berlin.de/wp-content/uploads/HTW-aquu-Stromspeicher-Inspektion-2026.pdf)).
- Value asymmetry: 1 MW/2 MWh German battery ~155–260 k€/MW/yr ([pv magazine](https://www.pv-magazine.de/2026/01/23/erloespotenziale-fuer-stationaere-batteriespeicher-in-deutschland-sind-2025-zurueckgegangen/); [photovoltaik.sh](https://www.photovoltaik.sh/news/erloespotenziale-und-wirtschaftlichkeit-von-netzgekoppelten-grossbatteriespeichern-in-deutschland-2026)) vs 10 kWh home battery ~50–250 €/yr in Germany ([Neon](https://neon.energy/Neon-Dynamischer-Stromtarif.pdf)) and ~900–2,100 €/yr measured in NL 2026 ([thuisbatterijnederland](https://thuisbatterijnederland.nl/onbalansmarkt-thuisbatterij/)).

### Inferences
- First paying customer: a direct marketer, aggregator or C&I EMS vendor that already holds dispatch rights and telemetry, buying a shadow-mode optimiser benchmarked against its realised revenue (ISEA benchmark gap of 195–214 k€ vs ~155 k€ realised is the pitch). Zero hardware, zero TSO interaction, liability stays with the partner.
- Second wedge: V1G optimisation sold to a utility/CPO (OCPP) or via Enode-type telematics, where the §14a Module 1+3 stack lifts per-car value to ~440 €/yr and the integration is standardised.
- Residential batteries are attractive only in NL (and plausibly Iberia, unverified) and only via a HEMS/aggregator partner; in Germany the per-asset value after efficiency losses, warranty cycle caps and platform fees is too small to carry a direct-to-consumer software business.
- V2G is not a 2026 software wedge: hardware is OEM-specified, dispatch is contractually locked to the OEM's utility partner, and revenue is a capped availability bonus (720 €/yr) where optimiser quality barely moves the outcome.

### Gaps
- Could not verify any OEM VPP's contractual exclusivity (sonnen, senec, E3/DC, 1KOMMA5°, Sigenergy) or any aggregator's willingness to expose a third-party optimiser API; this is a partner-interview question, not a desk-research one.
- Iberia (Portugal/Spain) control-stack specifics are entirely missing from this note.
