## KQ0. Market plumbing: where prices become money (balancing-group settlement, reBAP, 15-minute products, intraday, Redispatch 2.0)

### Takeaway
Every supplier, Direktvermarkter and battery operator in Germany settles against the quarter-hourly reBAP through a balancing group (Bilanzkreis). The reBAP is an index-coupled, scarcity-modulated price (since Dec 2022 under ISHM/PICASSO-MARI logic) that in 2024 averaged +226 EUR/MWh (volume-weighted) in quarter hours where the system was short and -26 EUR/MWh where it was long, with single-quarter-hour maxima of ~15,000 EUR/MWh. Since 1 Oct 2025 the day-ahead auction is also quarter-hourly, so forecasting, day-ahead, intraday and imbalance now run on one 15-minute grid. Redispatch 2.0 (since Oct 2021) adds a forecast/planning duty for every plant >=100 kW and makes the ~890 DSOs responsible for the balancing-group and financial settlement of curtailments.

### Cited Findings
- Balancing-group mechanics (MaBiS): each quarter hour an over- or under-covered balancing group is settled at the cross-zonal uniform price reBAP; the price can be positive or negative, so both long and short positions can cost or earn; total payments equal the TSOs' cost of balancing the zone — [Wikipedia: Ausgleichsenergie](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ausgleichsenergie); [Amprion: Informationen zum Ausgleichsenergiepreis](https://www.amprion.net/Strommarkt/Bilanzkreise/Ausgleichsenergiepreis/Informationen-zum-Ausgleichsenergiepreis.html)
- reBAP calculation since 8 Dec 2022 (BNetzA BK6-21-192): AEP-Modul 1 is price-based on aFRR/mFRR platform prices (PICASSO, MARI); Modul 2 = former "Börsenpreiskopplung" (minimum spread of 25% / at least 10 EUR/MWh between the intraday index "ID AEP" and the AEP once |NRV balance| >= 500 MW, linear below); Modul 3 = former "Knappheitskomponente", a parabolic surcharge that kicks in when >= 80% of dimensioned reserve is activated; historic caps (AEP20, 2016) and the 3% wälzungs-correction were removed in June 2022; interruptible loads reinstated in the NRV balance Nov 2023 — [netztransparenz.de: Ausgleichsenergiepreis](https://www.netztransparenz.de/de-de/Regelenergie/Ausgleichsenergiepreis); [Amprion](https://www.amprion.net/Strommarkt/Bilanzkreise/Ausgleichsenergiepreis/Informationen-zum-Ausgleichsenergiepreis.html)
- Historic cap: since 2010 the AEP is capped at the highest settled energy price for secondary and minute reserve — [Amprion](https://www.amprion.net/Strommarkt/Bilanzkreise/Ausgleichsenergiepreis/Informationen-zum-Ausgleichsenergiepreis.html)
- Final reBAP is published by the 20th working day after the delivery month; quality-assured NRV balance/reBAP data from the 8th working day — [netztransparenz.de](https://www.netztransparenz.de/de-de/Regelenergie/Ausgleichsenergiepreis); [TransnetBW reBAP](https://www.transnetbw.de/de/strommarkt/bilanzierung-und-abrechnung/rebap)
- The four TSOs publish an "AEP-Schätzer" (estimator) no later than 30 minutes after each quarter hour; for 1 Jan–31 Mar 2026 its mean absolute error vs final reBAP was 3.07 EUR/MWh (median 0.68, 95th percentile 9.10; 2.7% of mean absolute reBAP) — [netztransparenz.de AEP-Schätzer](https://www.netztransparenz.de/de-de/Regelenergie/Ausgleichsenergiepreis/AEP-Sch%C3%A4tzer)
- 15-minute day-ahead: EPEX SPOT moved the day-ahead auction to quarter-hour products on 30 Sep 2025 (first delivery day 1 Oct 2025), after a postponement from June 2025 due to matching-algorithm load; 96 prices per day, hourly index kept as arithmetic mean; gate closure 12:00; legal basis EU 2019/943 and 2017/2195 — [Vattenfall Energy Sales](https://energysales.vattenfall.de/en/publications/articles/day-ahead-exchange-trading-in-15-minute-instead-of-60-minute-intervals); [Next Kraftwerke blog](https://www.next-kraftwerke.de/energie-blog/day-ahead-15-minuten-umstellung); [solarzubau.de](https://solarzubau.de/wiki/day-ahead-markt/)
- Redispatch 2.0: applies since 1 Oct 2021 (NABEG 2.0) to all plants >= 100 kW incl. renewables and CHP and any remotely controllable plant; DSOs must report measures and carry "die Verantwortlichkeit für den bilanziellen und finanziellen Ausgleich"; potentially all 890 DSOs affected; bilanzieller Ausgleich governed by BNetzA BK6-20-059; the BDEW transition solution has expired — [BNetzA Netzengpassmanagement](https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/DE/Fachthemen/ElektrizitaetundGas/Versorgungssicherheit/Netzengpassmanagement/start.html); [BDEW Redispatch 2.0](https://www.bdew.de/energie/redispatch-20/)
- Cost of congestion management (BNetzA Monitoringbericht 2025, chart "Kosten der Systemdienstleistungen": "Redispatch mit Marktkraftwerken" incl. renewables and compensation to BKVs under the BDEW transition solution) ≈ 2,699.9 M EUR in 2024 (2023: 2,462; 2022: 1,781; 2021: 590.2 M EUR) — chart values read from the PDF, so treat as approximate — [BNetzA Monitoringbericht Energie 2025 (PDF)](https://data.bundesnetzagentur.de/Bundesnetzagentur/SharedDocs/Mediathek/Monitoringberichte/MonitoringberichtEnergie2025.pdf). A secondary source gives total congestion-management cost >2.77 bn EUR in 2024 — [4initia newsletter](https://newsletter.4initia.de/batteriegrossspeicher-in-deutschland-zwischen-boom-und-buerokratie/)
- Negative day-ahead prices: 457 hours in 2024 — [4initia](https://newsletter.4initia.de/batteriegrossspeicher-in-deutschland-zwischen-boom-und-buerokratie/); 573 hours in 2025 (BNetzA, Jan 2026, as cited by a vendor blog) — [enyo-energy.de](https://enyo-energy.de/magazin/solarspitzengesetz-60-prozent-regel)
- EEG 2024 structure: 72% of EEG volume in market premium (Direktvermarktung), 12% in "sonstige Direktvermarktung", 16% fixed feed-in; EEG payments 18.5 bn EUR, avg 8.0 ct/kWh — [BNetzA Monitoringbericht 2025](https://data.bundesnetzagentur.de/Bundesnetzagentur/SharedDocs/Mediathek/Monitoringberichte/MonitoringberichtEnergie2025.pdf)
- EEG annual market values 2025: JW 8.932 ct/kWh; wind onshore 7.441; wind offshore 8.059; solar 4.508 ct/kWh — [netztransparenz.de Marktwertübersicht](https://www.netztransparenz.de/de-de/Erneuerbare-Energien-und-Umlagen/EEG/Transparenzanforderungen/Marktpr%C3%A4mie/Marktwert%C3%BCbersicht)

### Inferences
- "Where prices become money": (1) supplier/BKV pays |imbalance| x reBAP each quarter hour; (2) Direktvermarkter pays the same on its EEG balancing group plus loses the market premium at negative prices; (3) a battery earns the day-ahead/intraday spread plus FCR/aFRR capacity and aFRR energy; (4) a curtailed plant under Redispatch 2.0 is compensated cost-based by the DSO/TSO, not market-based.
- The near-real-time AEP-Schätzer (MAE ~3 EUR/MWh within 30 min) means a model that merely "predicts reBAP after the fact" has little value; the money is in (a) forecasting the sign/size of the NRV balance before gate closure and (b) reducing one's own quarter-hourly error now that day-ahead is also 15-minute.

### Gaps
- Quarter-hourly reBAP time series for 2025–2026 could not be downloaded (netztransparenz data centre needs an API token; the TransnetBW page showed "data cannot be retrieved"). Need: netztransparenz WebAPI export or the 2026 Monitoringbericht (due ~Nov 2026) for 2025 annual volume-weighted values.

## KQ1. Balance-group economics: reBAP behaviour 2024–2026, typical Stadtwerk imbalance cost, value of a 10–20% forecast-error improvement

### Takeaway
Primary data exist only up to 2024: volume-weighted reBAP was +225.97 EUR/MWh in short-system quarter hours and -26.42 EUR/MWh in long-system quarter hours (2023: +240.3 / -38.73; 2022: +454.78 / -16.54), with a 2024 maximum of 14,999.99 EUR/MWh. No published figure for "a typical Stadtwerk's annual imbalance cost" was found; the best available anchors are BET's 2021 statement that imbalance costs doubled in 2020 and Vattenfall's 2022 statement that self-balancing customers' costs rose more than fourfold vs 2019/20 at equal forecast quality. My own order-of-magnitude estimate (clearly labelled) puts the value of a 10–20% error reduction at roughly 15–40 kEUR/yr for a 100 GWh supplier and 150–400 kEUR/yr for a 1 TWh supplier.

### Cited Findings
- 2024 volume-weighted reBAP: "bei Unterspeisungen ... 225,97 Euro/MWh" (c. 6% below 2023); "bei Überspeisungen ... -26,42 Euro/MWh (2023: -38,73)" — [BNetzA Monitoringbericht Energie 2025, p. ~37 and Fig. 63](https://data.bundesnetzagentur.de/Bundesnetzagentur/SharedDocs/Mediathek/Monitoringberichte/MonitoringberichtEnergie2025.pdf)
- Longer series (Fig. 63, EUR/MWh, positive NRV balance / negative NRV balance): 2022 454.78 / -16.54; 2023 240.3 / -38.73; 2024 225.97 / -26.42; pre-2021 positive-balance values were ~53–81 EUR/MWh (2015–2019, historical) — [BNetzA Monitoringbericht 2025](https://data.bundesnetzagentur.de/Bundesnetzagentur/SharedDocs/Mediathek/Monitoringberichte/MonitoringberichtEnergie2025.pdf)
- Maximum reBAP by year (Tab. 29): 2020 15,859.10; 2021 3,804.59; 2022 11,443.11; 2023 13,609.40; 2024 14,999.99 EUR/MWh — [BNetzA Monitoringbericht 2025](https://data.bundesnetzagentur.de/Bundesnetzagentur/SharedDocs/Mediathek/Monitoringberichte/MonitoringberichtEnergie2025.pdf)
- Dec 2023 (historical month, illustrates distribution): 2,348 quarter hours with positive reBAP (mean 115.97, median 107.18, max 9,189.60 EUR/MWh) and 628 with negative reBAP (mean -37.19, min -207.58) — [Wikipedia: Ausgleichsenergie](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ausgleichsenergie)
- Simple-average reBAP (historical): ~35 EUR/MWh in 2019 and 2020, ~100 EUR/MWh in 2021, ~158 EUR/MWh Jan–Apr 2022; "self-managing balance-group customers saw costs rise more than fourfold within a few months compared with 2019/2020 ... bei gleicher Prognosegüte" — [Vattenfall Energy Sales, June 2022](https://energysales.vattenfall.de/publikationen/artikel/5-fragen-zu-ausgleichsenergiepreisen)
- BET (Nov 2021, historical): in 2020 forecast deviation of suppliers rose ~15% on average and imbalance costs roughly doubled vs prior years; price spikes >16,000 EUR/MWh after the balancing-energy market (Regelarbeitsmarkt) was introduced; BET sells a benchmarking tool for suppliers to compare forecast quality and imbalance costs with peers — [ZfK / BET](https://www.zfk.de/energie/strom/ausgleichspreise-stadtwerke-wappnen); [BET PDF](https://www.bet-consulting.de/fileadmin/redaktion/PDF/Veroeffentlichungen/2021/zfkplus-Kostenexplosion_bei_Ausgleichsenergie.pdf)
- Balancing volumes fell in 2024: average activated SRL+MRL ~129–139 MW in H2 2024 vs 301–346 MW in 2016–2018 — [BNetzA Monitoringbericht 2025, Fig. 62](https://data.bundesnetzagentur.de/Bundesnetzagentur/SharedDocs/Mediathek/Monitoringberichte/MonitoringberichtEnergie2025.pdf)
- Systematic vs random error: a Swiss BFE study finds long-run expected imbalance cost is zero for uncorrelated errors, but PV errors are weather-driven and correlated with the system imbalance, so PV-heavy portfolios carry systematic imbalance cost — [BFE study (PDF)](https://pubdb.bfe.admin.ch/de/publication/download/12362); TU Wien 2023 identifies wind/solar forecast errors as the main driver of system imbalance — [IEWT 2023 presentation](https://iewt2023.eeg.tuwien.ac.at/download/contribution/presentation/159/159_presentation_20230304_225201.pdf)
- Historical cost of wind/PV forecast error at TSO level: ~1–2 EUR/MWh (2014 data) — [Fraunhofer ISI ImpRES (historical)](https://www.isi.fraunhofer.de/content/dam/isi/dokumente/ccx/2016/impres/Monitoringbericht_2014_final.pdf)
- Stadtwerke must offer dynamic tariffs since 1 Jan 2025; EY/BDEW: "Schwankende Börsenpreise und ein stärkeres Verbrauchsprofil mit Lastverschiebungen erfordern eine präzisere Prognose und eine laufende Anpassung der Beschaffungsstrategie" — [EY/BDEW Stadtwerkestudie 2025 (PDF)](https://www.bdew.de/media/original_images/2025/06/02/stadtwerkestudie-2025-ey-und-bdew_150dpi.pdf)

### Inferences
- Estimate (my arithmetic, assumptions unsourced): take 2024 reBAP of ~226 EUR/MWh when the system is short vs an energy value of ~90 EUR/MWh (2025 EEG JW 8.93 ct/kWh as a rough spot proxy) → a short BKV in a short system pays ~135 EUR/MWh over market; a long BKV in a long system dumps at -26 and forgoes ~90 → ~115 EUR/MWh. If roughly half of a supplier's absolute imbalance is "wrong-direction" and the other half roughly earns the spread, the expected net cost is ~50–70 EUR/MWh of absolute imbalance. Assuming absolute quarter-hourly imbalance of 2–4% of load (a typical range for retail portfolios, to be verified in interviews), a 1 TWh/yr supplier carries ~20–40 GWh of imbalance → ~1–2.8 MEUR/yr gross imbalance cost; a 10–20% error reduction is worth ~150–400 kEUR/yr net to that supplier (100 GWh supplier: ~15–40 kEUR/yr). This is the maximum a rational buyer would pay before internal costs and before the reBAP's own noise.
- Implication for pricing: a point forecasting tool is only a six-figure product for suppliers >= ~1 TWh of load or for pooled portfolios (Trianel, Syneco, Südweststrom), not for the median Stadtwerk (the EY/BDEW sample is 59% towns of 20–50k inhabitants).
- The 2024 asymmetry (short-system price ~9x the absolute long-system price) means under-forecasting load / over-forecasting own PV is far costlier than the reverse; a supplier should bias forecasts long. This is itself a sellable insight (quantile/asymmetric-loss forecasting).

### Gaps
- No published "typical Stadtwerk imbalance cost" (EUR/yr or EUR/MWh of load) was found from BDEW, VKU, Energy Brainpool, enervis, BET, con|energy or Enet (search budget exhausted before these could be queried individually). The BET benchmarking tool and Trianel's "customer reporting" (>40 users) hold this data; an interview with a Trianel/Syneco/Südweststrom portfolio manager or a BET consultant would reveal typical EUR/MWh imbalance cost and MAPE.
- 2025 and 2026 reBAP statistics are not yet in a primary publication; the AEP-Schätzer statistics only show the estimator error, not price levels.

### Inferences
- Hypothesis: Stadtwerke rarely buy a stand-alone forecasting model; the forecast is embedded in the EDM/PFM suite (Kisters/Seven2one/SOPTIM) or outsourced with the balancing group to a cooperative. - The lack of public tenders is consistent with procurement under the EU sector threshold (432 kEUR net from 2026, see KQ8) via direct award or framework call-offs, i.e., deals are won by relationship and pilot, not tender.

## KQ3. Shared-service / cooperative structures (Trianel, Thüga/Syneco, Südweststrom, KOM-Strom, 8KU, VKU, GETEC): do they centralise forecasting and trading, and are they buyers of models?

### Takeaway
Yes for Trianel (53 Stadtwerke shareholders, 138 TWh power traded in 2025, 2,900 MW direct marketing, 200 managed customer portfolios), Syneco/Thüga (Munich; ~100 partner utilities in the Thüga group) and Südweststrom (62 shareholders, Tübingen): all three run the balancing groups and short-term forecasts for their member Stadtwerke, i.e., they concentrate exactly the forecast error a model would reduce. 8KU is a Berlin lobbying office of the eight largest municipal utilities, not an operational buyer. KOM-Strom's domain is for sale (status unclear).

### Inferences
- The cooperatives are both the most attractive and the hardest buyers: attractive because one integration touches 50–100 Stadtwerke portfolios and they have in-house forecasting teams who can evaluate a model; hard because they view forecasting as core IP ("in-house AI tools", Trianel) and have long vendor relationships. The realistic wedge is a benchmark/second-opinion model on a subset of balancing groups (e.g., PV-heavy or heat-pump-heavy ones) with a shared-savings fee.
- Thüga/Syneco being in Munich is the single most reachable cooperative for a Munich founder; N-ERGIE (Nuremberg) is simultaneously a Thüga owner (20.53%) and a Thüga partner.

### Gaps
- GETEC and KOM-Strom status not verified (GETEC not fetched; KOM-Strom site dead). Whether cooperatives have ever bought an external model (vs. consultants) is unknown; ask Trianel's Leiter Portfoliomanagement or Syneco's Leiter Prognose.

## KQ4. Direktvermarkter: 2026 market structure, PV/wind forecasting and intraday needs, effect of Solarspitzengesetz, curtailment and negative prices, external model purchases

### Takeaway
As of 1 Jan 2026 the top seven are Quadra (10,100 MW), EnBW (9,900), Next Kraftwerke (8,020), Statkraft (6,800), Danske Commodities (6,400), BKW (6,180) and MVV (5,800); 46 companies answered the ZfK survey and 29 of them run battery portfolios. ## KQ5. C&I and large batteries: the 2025–2026 boom, what optimisers earn, and whether small C&I batteries (100 kW–5 MW) and co-located PV+storage are underserved

## KQ6. Industrial demand response: AbLaV status, §19 StromNEV reform, peak-shaving economics, who sells it

### Takeaway
AbLaV expired on 1 July 2022; the §19(2) StromNEV special network charges (Bandlast and atypical use) are being replaced through the BNetzA "AgNes" framework decision, with the Bandlast rule extended for existing customers to 31 Dec 2031, a framework decision due end-2026, the industrial design decided in early 2027 and the existing StromNEV rules expiring 31 Dec 2028. The signalled direction is a capacity price (EUR/kW/yr) for consumers >100,000 kWh/yr with a per-kWh surcharge above booked capacity, which would make peak forecasting the core of every industrial energy manager's cost model. Sellers today are Esforin (2.5 GW), Next Kraftwerke and N-ERGIE (industrial flexibility in DV portfolios), Energy Pool (8 GW global, no German detail found); the German "Entelios" demand-response business could not be traced (the entelios.com domain now belongs to Nordic Å Entelios).

## KQ7. DSO side: Redispatch 2.0 obligations, flexibility-market pilots, is there any paid, operating DSO flexibility procurement in 2026?

### Takeaway
No. As of October 2026 the evidence shows only (a) cost-based Redispatch 2.0 compensation for plants >=100 kW, (b) §14a EnWG dimming of controllable loads with network-charge reductions (not payments), and (c) publicly funded pilots: OctoFlexBW (TransnetBW + Octopus, >700 EVs, ~2 MWh/day of redispatch, published March 2026) and DataFleX (TenneT, TransnetBW, Avacon "Flexband", MVV/Beegy, OLI/Enorin, Fraunhofer, FfE; >5,000 devices; Oct 2025–early 2027; ~12 MEUR) that explicitly ask for a future "Redispatch 3.0" market. ## KQ8. Sales reality: how German utilities procure software, decision makers, sales cycle, and how small startups got their first paying utility customer

### Takeaway
Stadtwerke are Sektorenauftraggeber: above 432,000 EUR net (2026–2027 threshold) they must run EU-wide procedures (SektVO), below it national rules/direct awards apply, and contract value is the bundle (licence + implementation + related services). Public histories show the same pattern: enspired (founded Jan 2020) sold a first offering to prospects "before writing a line of code", took a German pumped-storage plant as first project and reached break-even in 2021 with grants and a 13-person team; suena and Entrix won first references with IPPs/municipal utilities (enercity, Prokon, MW Storage, MEAG) rather than through tenders.

### Cited Findings
- EU threshold for supplies/services by sector contracting entities (Stadtwerke): 432,000 EUR net from 1 Jan 2026 to 31 Dec 2027 (previously 443,000), Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/2152 — [BTL Rechtsanwälte](https://www.btl-recht.de/blog/vergaberecht-2026-neue-eu-schwellenwerte-fuer-auftraege/); contract value must include all functionally related supplies/services — [ibau.de](https://www.ibau.de/akademie/glossar/sektorenverordnung-sektvo/); [tender-service.de](https://www.tender-service.de/blog/sektorenverordnung-ueberblick); SektVO allows more flexible procedures and innovation partnerships — [evergabe.de](https://www.evergabe.de/glossar/sektorenverordnung-sektvo)

### Inferences
- A sub-432 kEUR pilot (typically 20–80 kEUR for a 6–12-month PoC, hypothesis) can be awarded directly by a Stadtwerk's Leiter Energiewirtschaft/Leiter Portfoliomanagement without a tender; the realistic gatekeeper at cooperatives is the Leiter Prognose/Portfoliomanagement, at Direktvermarkter the Leiter Handel/Head of Quant, at battery IPPs the Head of Asset Optimisation.
- All three battery-optimiser histories show the first customer came from (a) a specific asset owner with an acute P&L problem and (b) founders with prior trading-desk networks; none started from a Stadtwerk tender. For a founder without network, the most plausible first paying customer is a mid-sized asset owner (IPP/C&I) or a sub-contract to an optimiser/cooperative, not a Stadtwerk.
- Sales cycle length is not documented in any source; the enspired timeline (founded Jan 2020, break-even 2021) implies <12 months to first revenue for an asset-level product, versus multi-year for utility suites.

### Gaps
- No public data on utility PoC budgets or sales-cycle length; no source for "Leiter Energiewirtschaft" decision authority thresholds. Entrix and Flexpower founding/funding histories were not retrievable (404 on about/news pages). Interviews needed: a Stadtwerke Einkauf lead on direct-award limits (often internal thresholds like 25–100 kEUR), and founders at suena/Entrix on first-deal structure.

## KQ9. Candidate organisations (>=15), with websites, what they do, gap hypothesis, buyer role, visible external tech partnering; judged for a Munich-based ML founder with a Portuguese co-founder, no network, no capital

### Takeaway
The most reachable first buyers from Munich are (1) Thüga/Syneco (Munich, ~100 partner Stadtwerke), (2) Entrix and Kyon (both Munich, battery optimisation and IPP), (3) Stadtwerke München / SWM's ~2.4 GW direct-marketing book, (4) Energie Südbayern (Munich, 50% SWM / 50% Thüga, 4.8 TWh power), and (5) N-ERGIE (Nuremberg, 13.9 TWh power sales, Thüga owner, markets industrial flexibility). The structurally best-fit buyers nationally are the cooperatives (Trianel, Südweststrom), mid-sized Direktvermarkter (Pure Energy, Gewi, Ane, Sachsenenergie, in.power) and battery optimisers (suena, enspired, FlexPower), because they concentrate forecast error and already pay for models.

### Inferences (founder-fit judgement)
- Ranked by probability of a first paid pilot within 6 months for a Munich ML founder with no network/capital: (1) Entrix/Kyon (Munich; concrete quant problems; Iberian expansion matches a Portuguese co-founder; deal = data/feature licence or consulting sprint 10–40 kEUR); (2) Stadtwerke Rosenheim (documented history of buying external Redispatch/EDM tooling; CHP+battery dispatch is a bounded problem; direct award well below 432 kEUR); (3) Syneco/Thüga (one integration reaches ~100 partners; but in-house forecasting pride → offer benchmark on a subset); (4) ESB/N-ERGIE (1–14 TWh books with dynamic-tariff duty; value 0.15–0.4 MEUR/yr at 1 TWh); (5) mid-sized Direktvermarkter (Pure, Gewi, Ane) for white-label PV forecasting; (6) SWM (large but slow procurement). Bayernwerk Netz, LEW and REWAG are low fit (E.ON-centralised procurement, no paid DSO flexibility).
- The only segment where a two-person team can sell a *model* rather than a *service* is to optimisers/cooperatives who already run 24/7 desks; to end customers (Stadtwerke, C&I) they must sell an outcome (imbalance cost reduction, peak-charge reduction) and will be benchmarked against the cooperative's bundled offer.

### Gaps
- No organisation page states whether it buys external forecasting models; this can only be verified by interviews or by reading Kisters/Seven2one/emsys reference lists (not public). Stadtwerke Regensburg (das Stadtwerk), Stadtwerke Ulm/Neu-Ulm and GETEC were not researched. Contract sizes and sales cycles remain undocumented everywhere; a Stadtwerke Einkauf interview and a Syneco/Trianel portfolio-manager interview are the two highest-value next calls.
