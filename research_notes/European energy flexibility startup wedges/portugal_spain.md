# Portugal and Spain (Iberian MIBEL market) as first-market candidates for a flexibility forecasting / dispatch-optimisation software startup

Research date: 11 October 2026. Scope: evidence note for Portugal (full depth) and Spain (full depth), ending in a first-/second-/not-now judgment per country and a comparison with a German-only entry. Team context: Munich-based ML/optimisation founder + Portuguese co-founder on the ground in Portugal, no capital, no customers.

Method note: ~30 web searches and ~45 fetches were run; the shared web-search budget was exhausted part-way, so several items (notably full-year 2025 counts of zero/negative price periods for the Portuguese zone, Mobi.E/EV smart-charging rules, named Spanish aggregators beyond Bamboo Energy, and Galp/REN innovation programmes) could not be closed and are listed as gaps. Heavy PDFs (OMIE monthly reports, ERSE annual market report 2025, ERSE self-consumption report, the E-REDES FIRMe contract, the EDP consultation response) were text-extracted locally and read directly. Prices are wholesale €/MWh excluding VAT unless stated; Portuguese retail figures from ERSE bulletins are also pre-VAT unless stated.

## PT-1. Price formation and volatility: MIBEL day-ahead/intraday 2024–2026, zero/negative prices in the PT zone, 15-min MTU, PT–ES splitting

### Takeaway
MIBEL is one price zone most of the time (PT–ES splitting 1–12% of periods depending on month, with a post-blackout spike in May 2025); the PT day-ahead average was 66.19 €/MWh in 2025 (+4% vs 2024) with ~70% relative volatility, strong solar-hour cannibalisation (Q1-2026 solar capture price 16 €/MWh vs 41.9 €/MWh average), and the full 15-minute MTU chain (intraday Mar-2025, imbalance settlement Apr-2025, day-ahead 1 Oct 2025) is in force. Intraday liquidity is thin (continuous ID ≈ 2.3% of day-ahead volume in PT in 2025; IDA auctions ≈ 14–17% in the Iberian market). No official count of zero/negative periods specific to the Portuguese zone for 2025 was found.

### Cited Findings
- 15-min MTU timeline: "os mercados intradiários passaram a funcionar em intervalos tempo de mercado de 15 minutos, em março de 2025, o mesmo sucedendo com a liquidação de desvios, em abril de 2025, e com o mercado diário, em outubro de 2025". ERSE granted REN a derogation keeping the intraday cross-zonal gate closure (IDCZGCT) at 60 minutes until 1 Jan 2029; CNMC granted the same in Spain. TERRE (RR platform) was closed by TSOs at end-2025 — [ERSE Relatório Anual Mercados 2025 (published 4 Aug 2026)](https://www.erse.pt/media/y0glwi30/relat%C3%B3rio_ce-2025_pt.pdf)
- REN Data Hub confirms: "antes de 1 de outubro de 2025, os valores são horários… A partir dessa data, o mercado passou a ter discriminação em intervalos de 15 minutos"; the page currently shows market separation in 8.0% of periods (PT) / 12.2% (ES) for the displayed period (Oct-2026 page state) — [REN Data Hub – Mercado](https://datahub.ren.pt/pt/eletricidade/mercado/)
- PT annual day-ahead average: 66.19 €/MWh in 2025 vs 63.45 €/MWh in 2024 (+4%); 2025 PT price was ~34% below the estimated marginal cost of a CCGT; volatility (std dev / mean) ≈ 70% in 2025, i.e. prices "oscilaram, em média, num intervalo entre os 20 EUR/MWh e os 113 EUR/MWh"; volatility flat 2024→2025 — [ERSE Relatório Anual Mercados 2025](https://www.erse.pt/media/y0glwi30/relat%C3%B3rio_ce-2025_pt.pdf)
- Splitting in 2025: ERSE notes "um aumento substancial do número de horas de separação de mercados, face a 2024", driven by May 2025 when the Government (Despacho n.º 153/MAEN/2025, 4 May) restricted PT–ES interconnection capacity after the 28 April blackout — [ERSE Relatório Anual Mercados 2025](https://www.erse.pt/media/y0glwi30/relat%C3%B3rio_ce-2025_pt.pdf)
- Monthly splitting data points: July 2025 PT–ES price differences in 1.2% of hours (congestion rent €329k); Sept 2025 in 7.1% of hours (rent €609,856); Aug 2025 5.9% (Globalsyde) / 7.1% (OMIE PT report cited in search) — [OMIE Informe mensual julio 2025 (ES)](https://www.omie.es/sites/default/files/2025-08/informe-mensual_072025_es.pdf); [OMIE Relatório mensal setembro 2025 (PT)](https://www.omie.es/sites/default/files/2025-10/informe-mensual_092025_pt.pdf); [Globalsyde Aug-2025](https://globalsyde.com/blog/analisis-mercado-electrico-espanol-agosto-2025/)
- Monthly averages: Sept 2025 day-ahead 61.12 €/MWh (ES 61.04, PT 61.19); Sept 2024 was 73.63 (PT); July 2025 70.06 €/MWh (PT 70.01); lowest monthly PT average in 2025 was April at 18.62 €/MWh (search snippet from OMIE PT report); May 2025 minimum price −20.62 €/MWh (snippet) — [OMIE Sept 2025 PT](https://www.omie.es/sites/default/files/2025-10/informe-mensual_092025_pt.pdf); [OMIE July 2025 ES](https://www.omie.es/sites/default/files/2025-08/informe-mensual_072025_es.pdf)
- Q1 2026: MIBEL PT average 41.9 €/MWh (−22.5% y/y); March 2026 hourly min −9.83 €/MWh, max 193.84 €/MWh; March 2026 solar captured price 16 €/MWh vs hydro 62 €/MWh; renewables-only hours: 166 in March, 571 cumulative Jan–Mar 2026 (avg MIBEL price in those hours 36.2 €/MWh); March 2026 saw solar curtailment during 48 non-consecutive hours (max 4,700 MW) and wind curtailment during 19 hours (max 4,200 MW) — [APREN Boletim Eletricidade Renovável março 2026](https://www.apren.pt/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Mar2026_Boletim.pdf)
- Intraday liquidity: Sept 2025 IDA auctions 3,288 GWh = 16.6% of day-ahead volume (avg 59.49 €/MWh, −2.8% vs DA); continuous intraday 483.6 GWh = 2.4% of DA (weighted avg 51.39 €/MWh, −15.9% vs DA). July 2025: IDA 14.1% of DA, continuous 2.7% — [OMIE Sept 2025 PT](https://www.omie.es/sites/default/files/2025-10/informe-mensual_092025_pt.pdf); [OMIE July 2025 ES](https://www.omie.es/sites/default/files/2025-08/informe-mensual_072025_es.pdf)
- ERSE: in 2025 PT continuous-intraday volume ≈ 2.3% of day-ahead (~2,300 GWh), up on 2024 "devido à evolução do nível de liquidez"; "Em 2025, não se registaram alterações substanciais no mercado intradiário contínuo"; bilateral contracting weight 8.8% (4.6 TWh) in 2025 — [ERSE Relatório Anual Mercados 2025](https://www.erse.pt/media/y0glwi30/relat%C3%B3rio_ce-2025_pt.pdf)
- Historical: Portugal's first negative prices were in 2024 (the pplware article is about 2024, not 2025) — [pplware, 2024](https://pplware.sapo.pt/motores/portugal-tera-pela-primeira-vez-precos-negativos-de-eletricidade/)
- Current-state illustration: on Sat 10 Oct 2026 the Iberian day-ahead showed six negative periods (11:00–17:00) — but that page reports the Spanish zone — [Democrata.es, 10 Oct 2026](https://www.democrata.es/pt/economia/preco-da-luz-hoje-sabado-10-de-outubro-seis-horas-em-negativo/)

### Inferences
- Because PT and ES clear at the same price in ~90%+ of periods, the Spanish solar-cannibalisation statistics (PT-ES-1 below: 784 zero/negative hours in 2024, 607 in H1-2025) are a good proxy for the PT zone, with the caveat that PT's higher hydro share can make PT slightly less negative in spring.
- Midday price collapse + evening peaks (March 2026 range −9.83 to 193.84 €/MWh) is the spread a battery/EV/community optimiser monetises; curtailment of 4.7 GW solar in March 2026 shows the physical oversupply is now reaching Portugal.
- The 15-min market and imbalance settlement are fully live, so forecasting products need native 15-min resolution now.

### Gaps
- No official count of zero or negative 15-min periods / hours in the Portuguese zone for 2025 or 2026 was found (ERSE's annual report does not tabulate it; OMIE PT monthly reports give min/max only). The report-writer should state it as "≈ same as Spain, ~600–800 h/yr 2024–2025" with this caveat.
- Intraday auction (IDA) liquidity specific to the PT zone is not separately reported in the sources read.

## PT-2. Retail pass-through: ERSE indexed/dynamic tariffs, regulated tariff share, E-REDES smart meters and 15-min data access

### Takeaway
Portugal has near-universal smart metering (99% / 6.4 million BTN installations with daily 15-min data at end-H1-2025), a residual regulated market (≈800k clients = 12% of clients, 5% of consumption, Jan-2026) and 40 active retailers; indexed hourly-OMIE offers exist (EDP sells one), but no official share of consumers on indexed/dynamic tariffs was found. Third-party access to 15-min data with consumer consent is regulated (RSRI Reg. 817/2023 + EU Reg. 2023/1162) via the E-REDES Balcão Digital/app.

### Cited Findings
- Retail structure, Jan 2026 (Portugal continental): free market 5,801,696 clients (87.9%) and 46,073 GWh annualised (95.1%); regulated 799,513 clients (−7,262 m/m) and 2,368 GWh; 40 active retailers (38 residential); EDP dominant with 59.1% of clients (−2.1 pp y/y) and 38.6% of consumption; HHI 3,764 (clients) / 1,991 (consumption); monthly switching: 110,959 switches within free market, 20,785 entries, 12,901 exits. Listed retailers include Acciona, Ágoraluz, Alfa, Audax, Axpo, CapWatt, EDP Comercial, Elergone, Endesa, Enforcesco, ENI Plenitude, Ezu, Fortia, G9Telecom, Galp, Goldenergy, Ibelectra, Iberdrola, JafPlus, Logica, Lusíadaenergia, LUZBOA, MEO Energia, Moeve, Muon, Naturgy, NOSSA, Oeneo, Petrotermica, Portulogos, Power Plus II, Propensalternativa, Repsol, River-Energy, Start Campus Energy, Suncore, Usenergy, Volton, Ynerluz, Zug Power — [ERSE Síntese Mensal Janeiro 2026](https://www.erse.pt/media/4t3a02p0/202601_ele_rel_ml.pdf)
- ERSE annual: regulated market fell from ~930k clients (2021) to <810k (2025), 15%→12% of clients, ~5% of consumption; free market ≈5.8M clients / 88%; ~780k clients on the social tariff (12% of all consumers) — [ERSE Relatório Anual Mercados 2025](https://www.erse.pt/media/y0glwi30/relat%C3%B3rio_ce-2025_pt.pdf)
- Consumers on the free market can request the "regime equiparado às tarifas transitórias" from their retailer or return to the regulated CUR; ERSE's July 2025 bulletin notes returns to the regulated market "decorre, sobretudo, da possibilidade de os consumidores residenciais… optarem pelo regime equiparado" — [ERSE Síntese Mensal Julho 2025](https://www.erse.pt/media/zdblsvu3/202507_ele_rel_ml.pdf); [ERSE Jan 2026](https://www.erse.pt/media/4t3a02p0/202601_ele_rel_ml.pdf)
- ERSE Q3-2025 offers bulletin: only consumer types 1 and 3 have offers cheaper than the regulated tariff; for type 2 "a Tarifa Regulada corresponde à melhor opção"; Q2-2025: 17 retailers beat the regulated tariff for type 1 — [ERSE Boletim Ofertas Comerciais T3-2025](https://www.erse.pt/media/ma0eqm31/t3-2025_boletimofertascomerciais-eletricidade.pdf); [T2-2025](https://www.erse.pt/media/3l1kvaqc/t2-2025_boletimofertascomerciais-eletricidade.pdf)
- Indexed/dynamic offers exist: EDP Comercial's "Oferta Indexada" prices energy at "o valor horário registado no mercado grossista de eletricidade" (OMIE PT hourly) for smart-meter customers, daily-average OMIE PT for others; formula K1 (market-linked) + K2 €/kWh + K3 €/day + ERSE access tariffs; residential 3.45–20.7 kVA — [EDP Oferta Indexada](https://www.edp.pt/particulares/energia/gas-eletricidade-oferta-indexada/)
- Coopérnico (cooperative retailer/producer): 7,177 members, 5,774 contracts, €2.49M invested; publishes a "Previsor de Preços"; no public statement of indexed vs fixed structure on the homepage — [Coopérnico](https://www.coopernico.org/)
- Smart meters: at end-H1-2025 "99% (i.e., 6,4 milhões)" of BTN installations in mainland Portugal had a smart meter integrated in the smart grid; services include remote reading and "disponibilização diária de dados de consumo e produção com detalhe de 15 minutos", remote power changes, alerts, and "acesso de terceiros aos dados, com consentimento do cliente"; governing regulation RSRI Regulamento n.º 817/2023; DL 15/2022 required full BTN integration by 2024; Madeira 2026, Azores 2028 — [ERSE Redes Inteligentes (updated 18 Feb 2026)](https://www.erse.pt/atividade/regulamentos-eletricidade/redes-inteligentes/)
- Consent for third-party data sharing is given "através do Balcão Digital ou da app da E-REDES", under the RSRI and EU Implementing Regulation 2023/1162 (retailer explainer) — [Repsol PT](https://www.repsol.pt/particulares/assessoramento/contador-inteligente-eletricidade/)
- E-REDES Balcão Digital shows the daily load diagram in 15-min blocks (registered consumption, production, injection) for ACC members — [E-REDES Collective Self-Consumption](https://www.e-redes.pt/en/collective-self-consumption)
- Regulamento n.º 987/2025 (ERSE, 13 Aug 2025) defines the load diagram as 15-min average power values and targets 100% coverage by end-2030 (search snippet; refers to BTE/other segments — verify) — [ERSE Reg. 987/2025](https://www.erse.pt/media/kuddupwe/dr-regulamento-n-987_2025.pdf)
- E-REDES open-data portal (OpenDataSoft) exists with smart-meter counts by district/municipality/parish; the landing page fetched lists no dataset details — [E-REDES Open Data – Rede Inteligente](https://e-redes.opendatasoft.com/pages/redes-inteligentes/?flg=pt-pt)

### Inferences
- Data access is not the bottleneck in Portugal: a consent-based 15-min feed from E-REDES for ~99% of LV points is better than Germany's (where smart-meter rollout is far behind). This makes small-asset forecasting technically feasible nationwide.
- Retail is EDP-dominated (59% of clients) with a long tail of ~35 small retailers; the small retailers are plausible buyers of imbalance/forecasting tools but have small volumes.
- Dynamic pricing demand-side value exists (EDP hourly indexed) but there is no evidence of mass adoption; the regulated-tariff regime still competes well on price for small consumers, which blunts dynamic-tariff uptake.

### Gaps
- No official statistic on the share of consumers on indexed or dynamic tariffs in Portugal (ERSE bulletins include indexed offers in price comparisons but do not report uptake).
- Exact API/process for third-party 15-min data pull via E-REDES (beyond consent in Balcão Digital/app) not verified from a primary E-REDES page.

## PT-3. UPAC, collective self-consumption (ACC) and renewable energy communities (CER): DL 15/2022 + 2024–2026 amendments, numbers, E-REDES settlement, grid-fee treatment, vendors, and whether operators pay for optimisation

### Takeaway
Self-consumption is large (≈237k UPAC, 1.85–2.1 GW end-2024, ~2.8% of consumption) but collective schemes are small and only now scaling: 331 ACC at end-2024 → 410 (Jul-2025) → ~2,020 (Jul-2026) per DGEG, almost all using proportional sharing settled automatically by E-REDES; dynamic sharing exists only in one pilot ACC. DL 130/2026 (June 2026) and Lei 29/2026 (90-day licensing) ease the regime. CIEG exemption on shared energy was 100% through 2025 (7-year horizon per project). Vendors (Cleanwatts/Kiplo, Greenvolt Comunidades, EGACs) run asset-ownership/PPA-like models; no evidence was found that any Portuguese community operator pays a third party for forecasting/optimisation.

### Cited Findings
- UPAC scale: end-2024 ≈ 237k self-consumers and 1.8 GW (ERSE) / 2,116 MW UPAC under prior control, 99% PV (DGEG); UPAC count tripled 2021→2024 (78,841→236,888) and capacity ×3.9 (478→1,846 MW); 88% of UPAC ≤4 kW (19% of MW); 4–30 kW "mera comunicação prévia"; 30 kW–1 MW prior registration; >1 MW licence; self-consumption ≈ 2.8% of final consumption (1.5 TWh); surplus ≈ 27% of UPAC production, "em parte, vendidos em mercado organizado, através dos agregadores, mas a maior parte foi entregue à rede sem valorização" — [ERSE Relatório do Autoconsumo 2024 (approved 15 Jul 2025)](https://www.erse.pt/media/fkwdrz13/relat%C3%B3rio-sobre-o-autoconsumo.pdf)
- Data quality: of 29,161 UPAC >4 kW that must have a production totaliser meter, only 66% had one and only 22% (~6.5k) deliver reliable metering data — [ERSE Relatório do Autoconsumo 2024](https://www.erse.pt/media/fkwdrz13/relat%C3%B3rio-sobre-o-autoconsumo.pdf)
- ACC count and sharing modes: first ACC in Q4-2023; end-2024: 331 ACC on the mainland with 1,352 participating consumers (955 without own UPAC), avg 4 installations per ACC; Lisbon and Porto >80 participants each; 57% of municipalities have none; sharing "quase exclusivamente… proporcionais ao consumo"; only one ACC uses dynamic sharing (E-REDES pilot of dynamic + hierarchical sharing); proportional and fixed-coefficient sharing are processed automatically by the DSO; dynamic sharing makes the EGAC's role "substancialmente mais complexo" — [ERSE Relatório do Autoconsumo 2024](https://www.erse.pt/media/fkwdrz13/relat%C3%B3rio-sobre-o-autoconsumo.pdf)
- Growth 2025–2026 (DGEG data via press): ACC projects in operation 410 (Jul-2025) → 879 (start-2026) → 1,040 (Mar-2026) → ~2,020 (Jul-2026), +837 in June–July 2026 alone (+393% y/y); no CER count or MW given; PV capacity ~6.7 GW end-May-2026; PNEC target 20.8 GW (5.7 GW decentralised) — [Edifícios e Energia, 18 Aug 2026](https://edificioseenergia.pt/noticias/portugal-tem-quase-cinco-vezes-mais-projectos-de-autoconsumo-colectivo-num-ano/)
- DL 130/2026 (June 2026): eases joining/leaving, extends allowed distances between UPAC and consumption sites, sets ACC guiding principles, clarifies roles of DSOs/EGACs/administration, creates a national project map; partly in force from 28 Aug 2026. Lei 29/2026 (in force 1 Jul 2026): licences for renewable self-consumption units must be issued within 90 days, tacit approval otherwise — [Edifícios e Energia, 18 Aug 2026](https://edificioseenergia.pt/noticias/portugal-tem-quase-cinco-vezes-mais-projectos-de-autoconsumo-colectivo-num-ano/)
- Rules in force (pre-DL 130/2026 guide, April 2025): proximity = internal grid/direct line, or ≤2 km at LV, same substation at MV/HV, or ≤4 km (MV) / 10 km (HV) / 20 km (EHV) without same substation; sharing coefficients: fixed (weekday/weekend, seasonal), variable (hierarchical / proportional to measured consumption per ERSE interval), combinations, or "sistemas específicos de gestão dinâmica" that must access DSO meter data, return shared energy/coefficients to the DSO for deduction, and ensure interoperability; EGAC represents members and must sign a grid-use contract with the DSO — [Guia Legislativo Autoconsumo e CER, Apr 2025](https://poupaenergia.pt/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Guia-Legislativo-versao-revista_ABR2025-.pdf)
- Grid-fee treatment: CIEG (policy costs) on self-consumed energy transported via the public grid can be deducted from access tariffs by annual ministerial despacho (by 15 Sept). Exemption was 50% (individual) / 100% (ACC, CER) in 2021–22; Despacho 1177/2024 set 100% for all new ACI/ACC/CER projects licensed 1 Jan 2023–end-2024, for 7 years from start of operation; extended to 31 Dec 2025 by Despacho 1393/2025 — [Guia Legislativo, Apr 2025](https://poupaenergia.pt/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Guia-Legislativo-versao-revista_ABR2025-.pdf). ERSE confirms storage and self-consumption via the public grid pay access tariffs net of CIEG to avoid double payment — [ERSE Relatório Anual Mercados 2025](https://www.erse.pt/media/y0glwi30/relat%C3%B3rio_ce-2025_pt.pdf)
- E-REDES process: coefficients are set at registration; the EGAC may adjust them after certification via the "Self-Consumption Manager" reserved area; four types named (fixed, proportional, hierarchical, dynamic); a pilot studies hierarchical and dynamic coefficients; the DSO "calculates the energy to be shared… and issues invoices"; Regulation 373/2021 and 815/2023 apply; >1 MW needs production + operating licences — [E-REDES Collective Self-Consumption](https://www.e-redes.pt/en/collective-self-consumption)
- Public money: PRR C13 (Fundo Ambiental) "Apoio à concretização de CER e ACC": 38 M€ total after +8 M€ reinforcement (13 Feb 2026); beneficiaries CER, self-consumers, EGACs; eligible: renewable production with or without storage; execution deadline extended to 31 Dec 2026 (notice 30 Jun 2026) — [Fundo Ambiental C13-i01/02/03](https://www.fundoambiental.pt/apoios-prr/c13-eficiencia-energetica-em-edificios/c13-i01-02-03-apoio-a-concretizacao-de-comunidades-de-energia-renovavel-e-autoconsumo-coletivo.aspx). Government (13 Jun 2025): 112 ACC/CER projects approved, €19.1M, beneficiaries citizens, companies, municipalities, universities, public institutions — [Portugal.gov.pt, 13 Jun 2025](https://www.portugal.gov.pt/pt/gc25/comunicacao/comunicado?i=mais-de-19-milhoes-de-euros-para-112-projetos-de-autoconsumo-coletivo-e-comunidades-de-energia)
- Vendors: Cleanwatts (Coimbra) — Kiplo® EC platform "A plataforma líder para comunidades de energia renovável e autoconsumo coletivo"; a DWS infrastructure fund became shareholder with ~€150M investment plan to 2030; model = Cleanwatts installs PV at client sites with no client capex, sells energy at a "custo competitivo e previsível", anchors/members pay less than individual self-consumption, offers "otimização e monitorização das centrais para maximizar a produção", and plans batteries to cut costs — [Ambiente Magazine, 23 Feb 2026](https://www.ambientemagazine.com/dws-investe-150-milhoes-de-euros-na-cleanwatts-para-promover-autoconsumo-coletivo-ate-2030/); [Cleanwatts](https://cleanwatts.energy/empresas-energia/)
- Greenvolt Comunidades: "nós financiamos 100% do investimento", producers host panels, consumers join without changing retailer, claimed savings "-20 a 30%"; an EGAC handles technical/legal/operational management via a digital platform; no mention of batteries, forecasting or optimisation; recent projects: three Widerproperty shopping centres, two Hovione communities in Lisbon — [Greenvolt Comunidades](https://comunidades.greenvolt.com/)
- Coopérnico: cooperative, 7,177 members, 5,774 contracts; one opinion piece says it built seven units in a year with €403k — [Coopérnico](https://www.coopernico.org/); [A Caravela (opinion)](https://acaravela.substack.com/p/o-telhado-do-vizinho-ja-e-uma-central)
- In Porto the ACC managing entity is a regional energy agency; Cleanwatts and Greenvolt Comunidades developed commercial ACC products (civic working document) — [OS230 proposal doc, Feb 2025](https://www.os230.pt/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Proposta-11_03-Observar-Informar-Facilitar-e-Apoiar-Medidas-para-democratizar-e-acelerar-o-autoconsumo-e-as-comunidades-de-energia-renovavel.docx.pdf)
- Smartwatt (Porto, Grupo Gascan Energyco): energy management/efficiency/PV/O&M + "SAVING IS ON EMS" platform; 24 client logos (EDP, CTT, Bosch, Sonae…); no forecasting/community/battery offer on the site; its AI unit was spun off as Enlitia in May 2023 (production/consumption forecasting for solar/wind) — [Smartwatt](https://smartwatt.pt/); [ECO, 27 May 2023](https://eco.sapo.pt/2023/05/27/costela-de-inteligencia-artificial-da-portuense-smartwatt-da-origem-a-enlitia/)

### Inferences
- The settlement design removes most of the optimisation need: with proportional coefficients the DSO allocates production automatically each 15 min; an optimiser only adds value where (a) dynamic coefficients are used (one pilot ACC today), (b) a community battery exists, or (c) the EGAC wants to sell surplus in market via an aggregator. Today's money flow is PPA-like (Cleanwatts, Greenvolt) — vendors earn from energy sales, not from software.
- The 2025–2026 explosion in ACC count (×5) plus DL 130/2026's dynamic-management provisions and Cleanwatts' stated battery plans create a 2027+ opportunity for dynamic-sharing/battery optimisation, but the buyer would be two or three platform companies (Cleanwatts, Greenvolt, Smartwatt/Enlitia), not thousands of communities.
- Poor production metering (22% reliable) is a data-quality hazard for any forecasting product aimed at UPAC/ACC.

### Gaps
- No DGEG count of CER (as legal entities) separate from ACC; no MW for ACC.
- No evidence on whether Cleanwatts, Greenvolt or EGACs buy external forecasting/optimisation; Cleanwatts' Kiplo internal capabilities unknown.
- Post-2025 CIEG exemption status (despacho for 2026) not verified.
- DL 130/2026 text not read directly (summary from press).

## PT-4. Flexibility and aggregation: REN balancing services, independent aggregators, demand response, E-REDES flexibility (FIRMe), 2025–2026 storage/hybridisation tenders, home batteries, EV charging, blackout aftermath

### Takeaway
REN runs aFRR (secondary band + energy), mFRR (standardised since Mar-2024, on MARI since Dec-2024) and RR; aggregation of <1 MW units and a day-ahead mFRR capacity product were written into the MPGGS in 2025 (Diretiva 9/2025, after CP 127) with an "uncorrected" aggregation model; Ignis Energia was the first authorised independent aggregator (Feb-2025, renewable plants, not demand). Renewable+battery band enabled for balancing jumped from ~375 MW to ~1,250 MW in 2025. Regulation costs doubled to 13.06 €/MWh in 2025. Storage is tiny (~120 MW incl. near-commissioning mid-2025; 24 MWh standalone) but a 750 MVA standalone + 300 MVA co-located grid-capacity auction was slated for 14 Sept 2026 (launch not confirmed), the PRR BTM battery scheme funds ~500 MW/41 projects, and EDP's 180 MW BigBATT is due 2026. E-REDES has a FIRMe local-flexibility standard contract (≥10 kW, 15-min metering, availability €/MW/h + utilisation €/MWh, aggregators eligible, no penalties "at this stage"). ### Inferences
- Aggregation rules for <1 MW assets now exist on paper (Diretiva 9/2025) but the only authorised independent aggregator (Ignis) aggregates utility-scale renewables; no demand-side or residential aggregator is evidenced as active. ## PT-5. Imbalance (desvios) exposure of retailers and renewable producers, 2024–2026, and in-house vs bought forecasting

### Takeaway
Portugal uses a dual-direction, proportional imbalance allocation on 15-min periods since April 2025: each deviating BRP pays its share of the aFRR/mFRR/RR energy actually used in that period; total deviated energy was 5,478 GWh in 2025 (up from 5,305 GWh), regulation charges ≈13 €/MWh of consumption, and retailers pass system-services costs through (≈0.01 €/kWh "CGS e desvios" line in ERSE's example bill). No published €/MWh imbalance price series or any statement by named PT retailers/producers about buying external forecasts was found; the only concrete evidence of external forecast procurement in PT is TotalEnergies' Alentejo solar farms switching to Jungle AI (2024).

### Cited Findings
- Mechanism: imbalance value per hour "corresponde exatamente aos custos variáveis de regulação" paid to BSPs; costs of aFRR/mFRR/RR energy per imbalance settlement period are shared among deviating agents pro rata; total imbalance energy 5,305 GWh (2024) → 5,478 GWh (2025), with both short and long deviations up; 15-min imbalance settlement since April 2025 — [ERSE Relatório Anual Mercados 2025](https://www.erse.pt/media/y0glwi30/relat%C3%B3rio_ce-2025_pt.pdf)
- Costs of system-services markets fall on retailers (ERSE figure 3-1 "custos imputados aos comercializadores"), 9.58 €/MWh weighted average in 2025 — [ERSE Relatório Anual Mercados 2025](https://www.erse.pt/media/y0glwi30/relat%C3%B3rio_ce-2025_pt.pdf)
- Retail pass-through: ERSE's 2025 example invoice shows a "Custo de Gestão de Sistemas e desvios (CGS)" component of about 0.01 €/kWh (search snippet) — [ERSE Tarifas e Preços 2025 presentation](https://www.erse.pt/media/lw0ilxbu/tarifas-e-pre%C3%A7os-2025_v2.pdf)
- The CUR's (regulated supplier) purchase cost explicitly includes system services, profile adjustment and "os desvios decorrentes de aquisição do CUR em mercado" — [ERSE Tarifas e Preços 2026](https://www.erse.pt/media/irglmtt3/tarifas-e-pre%C3%A7os-se-2026.pdf)
- Producers: ~1,250 MW of solar/wind/BESS band enabled for balancing at end-2025 (vs 375 MW) indicates renewable producers are actively entering system services — [ERSE Relatório Anual Mercados 2025](https://www.erse.pt/media/y0glwi30/relat%C3%B3rio_ce-2025_pt.pdf)
- External forecasting evidence: Jungle AI (Toucan) replaced TotalEnergies' "existing forecasting provider" for TotalEnergies REN Portugal's Alentejo solar farms after a 3-month trial with "consistently… higher accuracy", citing reduced "energy market penalties" (26 Sept 2024) — [Jungle AI blog](https://www.jungle.ai/blog-posts/jungle-expands-power-forecasting-service-to-totalenergies)
- Surplus from UPAC is "em parte, vendidos em mercado organizado, através dos agregadores" (agregador de último recurso and market aggregators) — [ERSE Relatório do Autoconsumo 2024](https://www.erse.pt/media/fkwdrz13/relat%C3%B3rio-sobre-o-autoconsumo.pdf)
- Smartwatt spun off Enlitia (2023) to sell AI forecasting of production/consumption to renewables; REN publishes its own wind/solar forecasts every 15 minutes on the Data Hub (search snippets) — [ECO, May 2023](https://eco.sapo.pt/2023/05/27/costela-de-inteligencia-artificial-da-portuense-smartwatt-da-origem-a-enlitia/); [REN Data Hub](https://datahub.ren.pt/)

### Inferences
- Imbalance exposure in PT is real but modest per MWh (regulation charges ~13 €/MWh; imbalance energy ~10% of consumption) and is dominated by a few large BRPs (EDP, Galp, Iberdrola, Endesa). Small retailers (~35 with <5% share) have small absolute exposure, so willingness to pay for forecasting is bounded — a few k€/month at best.
- Renewable producers in market regime are the stronger forecasting buyer: TotalEnergies' switch shows they already pay external vendors and will churn on accuracy; Iberia-wide representation houses (Ignis, Axpo, etc.) bundle forecasting with representation, which is the real competitor.

### Gaps
- No €/MWh time series or annual average of PT imbalance prices (REN publishes "preço de desvio" data; not retrieved).
- No statements from Greenvolt, Finerge, EDPR, Iberdrola PT, Capwatt, Voltalia or Smartenergy on in-house vs external forecasting.
- No cost figure for retailers' imbalance costs separate from total system-services costs.

## PT-8. Judgment for Portugal

### Takeaway
Portugal is a credible *beachhead for learning and references* but a weak *first revenue market*: data access (99% smart meters, 15-min data), a 15-min market, aggregation rules on paper, strong solar cannibalisation and a post-blackout storage push are all real; but the flexible-asset base is tiny (≈120 MW BESS mid-2025, one renewable-only independent aggregator, ~2,000 ACC with automatic proportional sharing), the buyer list is short and EDP-dominated, and no PT community operator or retailer is evidenced paying for optimisation. Verdict: **first market for pilots and design partners (via the co-founder), second market for revenue** — unless the 1,050 MVA auction and PRR BESS cohort create a 2027 wave of BESS owners needing aFRR/mFRR bidding tools, in which case utility-scale BESS optimisation in Portugal becomes the wedge.

### Cited Findings

## ES-2. Spain — flexibility access: SRAD auctions, independent aggregator regulation (RD 88/2026, CNMC), demand/storage in secondary/tertiary, capacity market, blackout aftermath

### Takeaway
Demand response in Spain is real money today only via SRAD (REE's demand-response service): 1,148 MW at 56.43 €/MW·h for 2025 (zero activations, €283M availability cost), 1,725 MW at 65 €/MW·h for H1-2026 and 1,775 MW at 42.62 €/MW·h for H2-2026, 8 activation days in 2026; from 2026 consumers can participate aggregated through a retailer at ≥1 MW. RD 88/2026 (in force 12 Feb 2026) finally defines the independent aggregator with a centralised transitional compensation model, but as of Sept 2026 none of the CNMC rule changes, REE operating procedure or ministerial order had been published, so independent aggregators cannot yet operate. The capacity market was created by Orden TED/966/2026 (in force 18 Sept 2026; pay-as-bid, ≥1 MW incl. aggregations of smaller units, batteries with firmness coefficients, contracts to 15 years), first auction date not set (industry expects 2027). Post-blackout, RDL 7/2025 was repealed by Congress (July 2025); ENTSO-E's final report (Mar 2026) blamed voltage-control gaps; REE's "reinforced operation" raised adjustment costs sharply.

## ES-6. Judgment for Spain

### Takeaway
Spain is the larger, more monetisable Iberian market: deepest solar cannibalisation (≈600–800 zero/negative hours per year), a paid demand-response service (SRAD, €180–280M/yr availability) now open to aggregated consumers via retailers at ≥1 MW, independent aggregation legally defined (RD 88/2026) and expected to become operable in 2027, a capacity market admitting demand aggregators and batteries, hundreds of retailers with real imbalance exposure, and a documented small-vendor success (Bamboo). Verdict: **Spain is the Iberian revenue market and should be the "second market" in sequence but the primary commercial target** — the Portuguese co-founder's MIBEL knowledge transfers directly (same OMIE rules, same 15-min timeline, harmonised balancing products), but the team lacks a Spanish local presence and faces Bamboo/Ignis/utility incumbents.

### Cited Findings
- Summarised from ES-1 to ES-5 (OMIE/El Periódico de la Energía; REE/ESIOS/srad.energy; BOE RD 88/2026; pv magazine on TED/966/2026; UNEF; Bamboo Energy coverage).

### Inferences
- Sequencing option: design partners and pilots in Portugal (REN/E-REDES/EDP, small indexed retailers, BESS cohort) → productised offer sold to Spanish mid-size retailers/SRAD providers and BESS owners once aggregation and capacity-market rules are published (2027).

## X-1. Comparison with a German-only entry (what Iberia adds and what the Portuguese co-founder changes)

### Takeaway
Relative to Germany, Iberia offers better data (99% smart meters in PT vs. Germany's slow rollout), a fully 15-min market since Oct-2025, deeper and more frequent negative prices, an operating paid demand-response service (SRAD) and a soon-to-start capacity market, and a thinner vendor landscape; it lacks Germany's large home-battery/EV fleet, its §14a/dynamic-tariff obligations, and its established aggregator/VPP ecosystem. The Portuguese co-founder changes language, meeting access and credibility in Portugal (and partially in Spain through MIBEL commonality), but not the small Portuguese asset base nor the Spanish incumbents.
