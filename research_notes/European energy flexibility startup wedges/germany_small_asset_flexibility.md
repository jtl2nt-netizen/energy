# Germany: residential / small-asset electricity flexibility as it operates in October 2026 (software-vendor perspective)

Research date: 11 October 2026. Method note: ~20 web searches and ~40 page fetches. The web-search budget was exhausted mid-task, so some items could only be checked by direct fetch of known URLs; several primary pages (BNetzA §14a consumer page, StromNZV §4 on gesetze-im-internet, sonnenFlat-direkt product page, LichtBlick SchwarmBatterie page, Tagesspiegel Background) were unreachable (403/404/503/rate-limit) and are listed as gaps. All claims below are dated; "historical" marks pre-2025 observations.

## Key question 1: Dynamic tariffs — obligation, uptake, providers, structure, and the smart-meter precondition

### Takeaway
The obligation (since 1 Jan 2025, §41a EnWG) that every supplier offers at least one dynamic tariff is in force, but settlement requires an intelligent metering system (iMSys), and only 5.9% of all metering points (3.17 m of 54.1 m) had one at 30 June 2026. Survey uptake of dynamic tariffs is ~6% of adults (YouGov/1KOMMA5°, June 2026), and Verivox's H1-2026 analysis found dynamic tariffs almost always more expensive than the cheapest fixed tariffs for a non-shifting household. The addressable "settled on spot" base is therefore low single-digit millions of meters at most, and far fewer actual dynamic-tariff contracts.

### Cited Findings
- Since 1 January 2025 all electricity suppliers must offer at least one dynamic tariff; before 2025 the duty applied only to suppliers with >=100,000 end customers — [cleanthinking, 29 Sep 2026](https://www.cleanthinking.de/?p=1073211); [Solarserver, 4 Dec 2025](https://www.solarserver.de/2025/12/04/bundesnetzagentur-macht-dynamische-stomtarife-transparent/). Legal basis §41a EnWG — [corrently.io](https://corrently.io/books/flexibilisierung-der-stromversorgung-2025-praxisnahe-analysen-fur-die-energiewirtschaft/page/verpflichtung-zu-dynamischen-stromtarifen-ab-2025/export/pdf).
- BNetzA is tasked with a yearly monitoring report on dynamic tariffs over ten years (market offer, consumer cost, price volatility) — historical description of the draft, [ZfK, 24 Jan 2021](https://www.zfk.de/politik/deutschland/pflicht-zu-dynamischen-tarifen-soll-kommen). The 2021 draft also required suppliers with >200,000 customers to offer dynamic contracts to iMSys customers without a "technical/economic feasibility" caveat (historical).
- iMSys rollout at 30 June 2026: 54,135,202 metering locations reported; 3,172,579 (5.9%) with iMSys; +~765,000 in H1 2026 (+1.5 pp). End-2025 figure corrected after double-counting; corrected 31.12.2025 figure 2,406,454 iMSys (4.4%). Pflichteinbaufälle (6–100 MWh/a plus §14a devices): 4,831,178 cases, 1,259,270 with iMSys = 26.1% (target was 20% by 31.12.2025). Supervisory proceedings since March 2026 against 434 metering operators that missed 20%, 77 at 0%, 150 closed. 90% of mandatory cases by 2032 — [BNetzA press release, 21 Sep 2026](https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/DE/2026/20260921_Rollout.html); [BNetzA iMSys page](https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/1033662); [elektroauto-news, 23 Sep 2026](https://www.elektroauto-news.net/news/bundesnetzagentur-korrigiert-smart-meter-zahlen-2025).
- From end-2026 the 20% target is replaced by "Zeitraumquoten" (period quotas); percentages not published in the sources found — [elektroauto-news, 23 Sep 2026](https://www.elektroauto-news.net/news/bundesnetzagentur-korrigiert-smart-meter-zahlen-2025).
- Smart-meter price caps (as of Sep 2026): €30/yr gross up to 6,000 kWh; €40 for 6,000–10,000 kWh; €50 with a controllable device; up to €100 one-off for early installation on request — [cleanthinking, 29 Sep 2026](https://www.cleanthinking.de/?p=1073211).
- Uptake: YouGov survey for 1KOMMA5°, 9–11 June 2026, n=2,074: 6% use a dynamic tariff; 49% willing to consume flexibly (12% very, 37% rather) — [1KOMMA5° (company-commissioned survey)](https://1komma5.com/de/magazin/news/umfrage-fast-die-haelfte-der-deutschen-zu-flexiblem-stromverbrauch-bereit-nur-sechs-prozent-nutzen-dynamische-tarife/). An older figure says ~7% of households used dynamic tariffs by end-2024 — [topagrar](https://www.topagrar.com/energie/news/flexible-stromtarife-werden-immer-beliebter-20017644.html). Note: survey "use" likely overstates contracts actually settled on iMSys given 5.9% iMSys penetration.
- forsa/vzbv (July/Oct 2024, historical): 53% of respondents did not know dynamic tariffs at all — [cleanthinking](https://www.cleanthinking.de/?p=1073211).
- Tariff structure: energy price tracks the day-ahead spot price (quarter-hourly day-ahead since 1 Oct 2025); other components (grid fees, taxes, levies, metering) are fixed. BNetzA publishes modelled dynamic household prices on SMARD (since 2 Dec 2025), weighting spot prices by a standard load profile and using the other components from its monitoring of dynamic-tariff providers; modelled dynamic prices were below modelled fixed tariffs every month from April 2025 for a non-shifting standard household — [Solarserver, 4 Dec 2025](https://www.solarserver.de/2025/12/04/bundesnetzagentur-macht-dynamische-stomtarife-transparent/); [cleanthinking](https://www.cleanthinking.de/?p=1073211).
- Verivox H1 2026 (published 16 July 2026): 3-person household 4,000 kWh/yr — dynamic €645 (€604 with 15% load shifted), Grundversorgung €787, cheapest fixed with bonus €481, without bonus €588. EV commuter 6,000 kWh/yr — dynamic €821, Grundversorgung €1,189, fixed with bonus €726, without €889. Dynamic tariff modelled with 0.731 ct/kWh net surcharge and €5.03/month base fee; Modul 1 flat grid-fee reduction of €75 net for H1 applied in EV case. Verivox: "Dynamische Stromtarife sind fast immer teurer als die günstigsten Festpreistarife"; time-variable grid fees "vielerorts aber noch nicht von den Stromnetzbetreibern umgesetzt" — [Solarserver, 16 Jul 2026](https://www.solarserver.de/2026/07/16/verivox-dynamische-stromtarife-teurer-als-festpreistarife/); [ZfK, 16 Jul 2026](https://www.zfk.de/unternehmen/dynamische-stromtarife-verivox-festpreis-netzentgelte-2026).
- Average household price 2026 YTD 37.0 ct/kWh (BDEW, Aug 2026): 15.2 ct procurement/sales, 9.2 ct grid fees, 12.6 ct taxes/levies. Day-ahead average €78.51/MWh (2024), €89.32/MWh (2025); negative-price hours 457 (2024), 573 (2025); hours >€300/MWh 41 (2024), 40 (2025); PV feed-in 63.2 TWh (2024), 74.1 TWh (2025) — [cleanthinking, citing BDEW/BNetzA](https://www.cleanthinking.de/?p=1073211).
- Verbraucherzentrale position: "Für normale Haushaltsstromkunden sind die Tarife in der Regel nicht empfehlenswert" — [cleanthinking](https://www.cleanthinking.de/?p=1073211).
- Named providers (as seen on their own pages, Oct 2026): Tibber "Smart Dynamic" (dynamic, requires smart meter) — [tibber.com/de/grid-rewards](https://tibber.com/de/grid-rewards); Octopus "Intelligent Octopus" is NOT a pass-through dynamic tariff but a fixed Arbeitspreis with a "SmartConnect Bonus" of 12 ct/kWh (EV) / 10 ct/kWh (battery) after smart-meter configuration, 6/5 ct transitional; iMSys mandatory, Octopus installs it free, install typically within 3 months then 2–3 months DSO configuration — [octopusenergy.de](https://octopusenergy.de/intelligent-octopus); 1KOMMA5° "Dynamic Pulse" ("zeitweise ab 0 Cent pro kWh", footnoted to May–Aug 2024 customer data) — [1komma5.com](https://1komma5.com/de/); LichtBlick "StromWallet Dynamic Pro" (dynamic tariff + EMS + metering) — [lichtblick.de](https://www.lichtblick.de/schwarmbatterie/) (redirected page); Enpal has its own tariff provider "Enpal Energy" and smart-meter subsidiary "Metrify" — [pv magazine, 18 Jun 2026](https://www.pv-magazine.de/2026/06/18/enpal-stellt-seine-infrastruktur-anderen-marktteilnehmern-zur-verfuegung/). Rabot appears in a July 2026 press release with clever-PV and inexogy (PDF unreadable) — [inexogy PDF](https://www.inexogy.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Pressmitteilung-clever-pv-rabot-inexogy.pdf).
- Monitoringbericht 2025 (BNetzA/BKartA, 26 Nov 2025) analyses 2024 data; households could choose among 139 electricity suppliers in 2024; the press release excerpts found contain no dynamic-tariff customer count — [BNetzA press release](https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/DE/2025/20251126_Monitoringbericht.html) (full page fetch was rate-limited).

### Inferences
- The binding constraint on any dynamic-tariff-based optimisation business is the meter, not the tariff: at ~765k new iMSys per half-year, the installed base will be roughly 4–5 m by end-2027, still under 10% of meters. Any model that needs 15-min settled data has a customer universe in the low millions, concentrated among PV/EV/heat-pump households (the Pflichteinbaufälle group, 26% equipped).
- Because grid fees and taxes are 21.8 of 37 ct/kWh, pure spot arbitrage by a household captures a small slice; Verivox's own numbers show load shifting of 15% of consumption is worth ~€41/yr. The commercial logic for suppliers is therefore device control (EV, battery, heat pump) rather than behavioural shifting.
- BNetzA collects supplier-level data on dynamic tariffs (it uses them for the SMARD model), so an official uptake count probably exists in the Monitoringbericht 2025/2026 full text; it was not retrievable here.

### Gaps
- No official count of households on dynamic tariffs in 2025/2026 was found; the Monitoringbericht 2025 PDF (BNetzA) and the SMARD dynamic-tariff page should be read directly. BNetzA domain fetches were rate-limited during this research.
- Number of DSOs actually offering Modul 3 (time-variable grid fees) and the number of §14a devices registered: not found in any source; BNetzA's 28 May 2026 enforcement press release and the Monitoringbericht are the places to check.
- Statista lists "Anzahl der Stromlieferanten von dynamischen Stromtarifen" (paywalled, not accessed) — [Statista](https://de.statista.com/statistik/daten/studie/1548434/umfrage/anzahl-der-stromlieferanten-von-dynamischen-stromtarifen).

## Key question 2: §14a EnWG — Modules 1–3, DSO dimming practice, monetisable flexibility for third parties

### Takeaway
§14a (Festlegung BK8-22/010-A "NSAVER", 23 Nov 2023) is in force: Modules 1 and 2 since 1 Jan 2024, Module 3 (time-variable grid fee, three price levels) mandatory for DSOs since 1 April 2025. In exchange DSOs may dim controllable devices to 4.2 kW in congestion. Implementation of Module 3 is patchy: BNetzA threatened its first Zwangsgelder against two DSOs (Syna, TEN Thüringer Energienetze) on 28 May 2026 with a 30 Sep 2026 deadline. §14a creates a regulated grid-fee rebate (€110–190/yr Module 1; 60% off the volumetric grid fee in Module 2) paid by the DSO to the connection user, not a market for third parties; the only third-party value is in optimising against Module 3 price windows.

### Cited Findings
- Modul 1: flat reduction €110–190 per year per Marktlokation (BNetzA range, 2023 basis); Modul 2: 60% reduction of the grid-fee Arbeitspreis, needs separate metering of the device; Modul 3: time-variable grid fee with three tariff levels, requires iMSys, only in addition to Modul 1; DSO may reduce the device to 4.2 kW but not switch off — [ema-energiewelt, 21 Aug 2026](https://ema-energiewelt.de/wissen/paragraph-14a-modul-3-netzbetreiber-verfahren-2026); Modul 3 price levels must be applied for at least two quarters of a year — [Haufe](https://www.haufe.de/recht/weitere-rechtsgebiete/wirtschaftsrecht/reduzierung-der-netzentgelte_210_642454.html).
- Modules 1 and 2 mandatory since 1 Jan 2024; Modul 3 since 1 April 2025; legal basis Festlegung BK8-22/010-A of 23 Nov 2023 — [ema-energiewelt](https://ema-energiewelt.de/wissen/paragraph-14a-modul-3-netzbetreiber-verfahren-2026).
- All DSOs legally obliged to offer Modul 3 since 1 April 2025, but "die praktische Verfügbarkeit variiert jedoch noch je nach Netzbetreiber" — [enter.de](https://www.enter.de/blog/14a-enwg-was-bedeutet-die-steuerbarkeit-meiner-waermepumpe); Verivox (July 2026): time-variable grid fees not yet implemented by many DSOs — [Solarserver, 16 Jul 2026](https://www.solarserver.de/2026/07/16/verivox-dynamische-stromtarife-teurer-als-festpreistarife/).
- Enforcement: on 28 May 2026 BNetzA issued its first Verwaltungsvollstreckungsmaßnahmen under NSAVER, with Zwangsgeld threats against Syna GmbH (BK8-26-001-S) and TEN Thüringer Energienetze (BK8-26-002-S); deadline 30 Sep 2026; BNetzA president Klaus Müller: "Wir stellen fest, dass Unternehmen insbesondere das zeitvariable Netzentgelt nicht oder nur unzureichend umsetzen" — [ema-energiewelt, citing BNetzA press release 28 May 2026](https://ema-energiewelt.de/wissen/paragraph-14a-modul-3-netzbetreiber-verfahren-2026).
- Tibber quantifies Modul 1 for an EV household at up to €190/yr (requires smart meter and charging box) — [tibber.com/de/grid-rewards](https://tibber.com/de/grid-rewards); Verivox used €75 net for H1 2026 — [Solarserver](https://www.solarserver.de/2026/07/16/verivox-dynamische-stromtarife-teurer-als-festpreistarife/).
- Smart-meter price cap with a controllable device is €50/yr — [cleanthinking](https://www.cleanthinking.de/?p=1073211).
- Octopus/TransnetBW "OctoFlexBW" pilot (June 2024 – March 2026, ~700 private EVs) tested EV flexibility for redispatch (congestion management): ~2 MWh/day retrievable from ~700 vehicles; TransnetBW extrapolates 1 m EVs to ~3 GWh/day or up to 0.5 TWh/yr (3–5% of German redispatch need); no participant payment reported (participants benefited from cheaper charging via Intelligent Octopus); follow-on project "DataFleX" to add >1,000 EVs and home storage; TransnetBW calls for a market-based "Redispatch 3.0" and a regulatory framework to recognise TSO call costs; Octopus DE CEO Bastian Gierull: "Alles, was wir dafür brauchen, ist eine freie Bahn in der Regulatorik und ein Smart Meter in jedem Haushalt" — [electrive, 26 Mar 2026](https://www.electrive.net/2026/03/26/octoflexbw-projekt-schwarmspeicher-aus-700-e-autos-erprobt/).

### Inferences
- §14a money flows DSO -> connection user as a regulated rebate; there is no aggregator role and no market price for the "dimming right". A third-party software vendor can only sell (a) Module-3-aware dispatch (shifting EV/heat-pump/battery charging into low-grid-fee windows), which is only valuable where the DSO has actually published Module 3 prices and the household has an iMSys, or (b) DSO-side tooling (forecasting local congestion, deciding when to dim), which is a DSO procurement question, not an aggregator one.
- The ~800 DSOs with heterogeneous, late Module 3 implementation means the "Module 3 optimisation" feature is a long tail of tariff tables rather than a model problem; the modelling value is small per customer (grid fee ~9 ct/kWh average, three levels, applied to a few kWh/day of shiftable load).
- Redispatch participation of small assets (OctoFlexBW/DataFleX) is still pilot-stage and uncompensated at the household level; a market-based "Redispatch 3.0" is a proposal, not a rule.

### Gaps
- No statistics on actual dimming events by DSOs in 2025–2026 were found; the sources state none exist publicly. BNetzA's NSAVER monitoring or DSO annual reports would be needed.
- Number of registered §14a devices (and share with Steuerbox) not found; the BNetzA §14a consumer page (https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/DE/Vportal/Energie/SteuerbareVE/start.html) could not be fetched (rate-limited/404).
- No list of DSOs with live Module 3 tariffs was found (ZfK has an article "zeitvariable netzentgelte modul 3 netzbetreiber", not fetched).

## Key question 3: Solarspitzengesetz (Feb 2025), EEG 2027 draft, and money in direct marketing of small PV and home batteries

### Takeaway
Since 25 Feb 2025 new PV installations get no feed-in payment in quarter-hours with negative day-ahead prices, must throttle feed-in to 60% without iMSys/Steuerbox, and batteries may legally be charged from the grid and discharged (multi-use) without losing EEG status; the cabinet's EEG 2027 draft (29 Jul 2026, in Bundestag since Sep 2026, planned 1 Jan 2027, EU state-aid approval pending) would end the 20-year fixed tariff for new small PV (36-month transitional ~5.2 ct/kWh, then mandatory direct marketing, 1.5 ct/kWh bonus for <25 kW for 48 months, permanent 50% feed-in cap). The direct-marketing rail is being built by vertically integrated players (Enpal targets 500,000 retrofit households by 2030; sonnen, 1KOMMA5°, Tibber), but no source publishes an independent €/battery/yr figure for Germany; the only hard household-level numbers are Tibber's Netherlands claims (~€1,020–1,810/yr) and Tibber's German EV-only Grid Rewards (up to €150/yr).

### Cited Findings
- Solarspitzengesetz in force since 25 Feb 2025: no feed-in tariff during negative prices for new systems; batteries may be charged with cheap grid power and discharged later; without smart meter and control box, feed-in throttled to 60% — [t-online](https://www.t-online.de/heim-garten/aktuelles/id_101367692/solaranlage-warum-sich-der-netzanschluss-noch-2026-lohnt.html); [clever-pv](https://clever-pv.com/en/post/solarspitzengesetz-neue-regelungen-fuer-pv-anlagen). Current small-PV tariff 7.78 ct/kWh (2026) — [Enpal](https://www.enpal.de/photovoltaik/einspeiseverguetung).
- Negative-price hours: 457 (2024), 573 (2025) — [cleanthinking](https://www.cleanthinking.de/?p=1073211). ~1.5 TWh of renewables curtailed in Germany Jan–Jun 2026 — [electrive, 11 Sep 2026](https://www.electrive.net/2026/09/11/tibber-startet-grid-rewards/).
- November 2025 EnWG amendment extended the grid-fee exemption to multi-use storage; commercial storage can now trade in addition to self-consumption/peak shaving — [pv magazine, 8 May 2026](https://www.pv-magazine.de/2026/05/08/starkes-wachstum-im-grossspeichermarkt-fuehrt-zu-batterie-rekordzubau-von-ueber-2-gigawattstunden-im-1-quartal-2026/).
- EEG 2027 cabinet draft (29 Jul 2026): fixed feed-in tariff for small rooftop PV abolished; direct marketing mandatory for all new plants; uniform support value 6.2 ct/kWh; CfDs for larger PV; feed-in capped at 50% at the connection; faster iMSys rollout for PV stated as aim; Bundestag deliberation from Sept 2026; EU state-aid approval needed; SPD demanded changes and decoupling from Netzpaket — [pv magazine, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.pv-magazine.de/2026/07/29/kabinett-beschliesst-entwuerfe-zu-eeg-2027-und-netzpaket/).
- Draft detail: transitional payment = 6.2 ct minus 1 ct = 5.2 ct/kWh for max 36 months after commissioning (eligible: commissioned 2027 <50 kW, 2028 <25 kW, 2029–2030 <7 kW); <25 kW gets no permanent support and must direct-market; >=25 kW gets Marktprämie; >=100 kW CfD; Direktvermarktungsbonus §50c: 1.5 ct/kWh on top of market revenue for <25 kW in "sonstige Direktvermarktung", max 48 months; permanent 50% feed-in cap. Timeline: Drucksache 21/7867 to Bundestag 7 Sep 2026, first reading 24 Sep, Bundesrat 25 Sep, hearing 5 Oct 2026; 2nd/3rd reading not scheduled as of 8 Oct 2026; planned entry 1 Jan 2027; systems commissioned by 31 Dec 2026 keep the fixed tariff; BSW-Solar (24 Jul 2026) warns of a de-facto direct-marketing obligation for small systems — [ema-energiewelt, 8 Oct 2026](https://ema-energiewelt.de/wissen/eeg-novelle-2027-kabinettsbeschluss-einspeiseverguetung-endet). Press report variants of the thresholds conflict — [infranken](https://www.infranken.de/ratgeber/karriere-geld/aus-fuer-einspeiseverguetung-pv-anlagen-direktvermarktung-stromfoerderung-eeg-art-6384795); [boerse-express](https://www.boerse-express.com/news/articles/eeg-novelle-foerderung-fuer-kleine-solaranlagen-sinkt-von-20-auf-3-jahre-935641). BEE criticism (28 Aug 2026) — [BEE position paper](https://www.bee-ev.de/fileadmin/Redaktion/Dokumente/Meldungen/Positionspapiere/2026/20260828_BEE_Kernpunkte_EEG_NAP.pdf). A study cited in press says small-plant direct marketing lacks standardised processes and smart meters, "und die Erlöse sind schlicht zu gering" — [energiezukunft](https://www.energiezukunft.eu/politik/kleine-solaranlagen-zukuenftig-ohne-foerderung).

### Inferences
- Direct marketing of small PV/batteries in Germany is on the verge of becoming compulsory for new systems (if EEG 2027 passes as drafted) but is being commercialised almost exclusively as a bundle: supplier contract + HEMS/inverter integration + smart meter + VPP by the same company (Enpal, 1KOMMA5°, sonnen, Tibber, Octopus). The direct marketer needs the iMSys, the control path and the Bilanzkreis; a software-only vendor has none of these.
- Published German household-level flexibility revenue is EV-only and small (Tibber: up to €150/yr intraday, €440 with smart charging, €630 with Modul 1). Battery figures of ~€1–1.8k/yr are Dutch, under a different grid-fee and tax regime, and are company claims. The German per-battery figure is probably much lower (grid fees and levies apply to grid charging; home batteries are ~8–11 kWh / ~5 kW) but no independent German number exists.
- The 36-month transitional tariff plus 50% cap in the draft creates a 2027–2030 cohort of several hundred thousand new PV systems per year that must find a Direktvermarkter; this is the clearest structural demand pull for "small-asset direct-marketing stacks", and it is being pre-empted by Enpal's "operations-as-a-service" and Flexa.

An interview with a Direktvermarkter (Next Kraftwerke, Interconnector, Flexa) would settle it.

## Key question 6: Imbalance (reBAP) exposure and whether retail aggregators can monetise imbalance optimisation; Bilanzkreis rules

### Takeaway
German balance-group rules oblige the Bilanzkreisverantwortlicher to keep the group balanced (schedule notification at the latest 15 minutes before delivery; reBAP settled per quarter-hour; TSO covers residual imbalances and bills the BKV), and the regulator has since 2019/2020 tightened "Bilanzkreistreue" (intraday schedule duties, reBAP tied to the volume-weighted quarter-hour intraday price, scarcity component). Passive balancing as a business model — the Dutch pattern — is not available in Germany; even in the Netherlands Tibber says it avoids the imbalance market. The primary legal text (StromNZV §4) could not be fetched; see Gaps.

### Cited Findings
- 50Hertz: BKVs are responsible for keeping the balance group settled via physical generation/withdrawal plus trades; if a BKV cannot balance, the TSO uses balancing energy and settles the cost with that BKV; day-ahead schedules due 14:30 D-1; reBAP published per quarter-hour; settlement under MaBiS — [50Hertz Bilanzkreiskunden](https://www.50hertz.com/Vertragspartner/Bilanzkreiskunden) (Standardbilanzkreisvertrag PDF version 1 Oct 2024 linked, not read).
- BDEW (12 Aug 2019, historical): BNetzA measures to strengthen Bilanzkreistreue covered intraday schedule management, reBAP calculation and data transmission; the balancing obligation must be met at the latest 15 minutes before delivery by schedule notification; the Bilanzkreisvertrag effective 1 May 2020 added intraday schedule duties; BDEW supported using the volume-weighted quarter-hour ID price as reBAP reference — [BDEW](https://www.bdew.de/energie/bdew-kritisiert-bnetza-ma%C3%9Fnahmen-zur-staerkung-der-bilanzkreistreue/).
- Statkraft (Sep 2020, historical): the reBAP scarcity component creates incentives to deliberately over-supply a balance group, which "widerspricht der Bilanzkreistreue" — a position paper arguing the design conflicts with the duty, not a statement that intentional imbalance is lawful — [Statkraft](https://www.statkraft.de/globalassets/0/.de/06-statkraft-germany/positionspapiere/202009_statkraft-zu-unb-knappheitskomponente.pdf). reBAP model descriptions (Amprion) — [Amprion](https://www.amprion.net/Dokumente/Strommarkt/Bilanzkreise/Ausgleichsenergiespreis/2022/Modellbeschreibung_der_reBAP-Berechnung_vom_01.08.2021-21.06.2022.pdf).
- Suppliers must themselves ensure Bilanzkreistreue even if they pass costs to customers — [Enpal lexicon](https://www.enpal.de/energie-lexikon/ausgleichsenergie).
- Flexa (Nov 2024): "Rebalancing for direct energy marketing is still being negotiated with grid operators" — i.e., the asset-side schedule/Bilanzkreis allocation for small assets was an open operational issue — [ESS News](https://www.ess-news.com/2024/11/05/germanys-enpal-announces-launch-of-virtual-power-plant/).
- Netherlands comparison: Tibber deliberately avoids the Dutch onbalansmarkt ("small market whose returns fall as more parties join") and trades home batteries on the pan-European intraday market; quoted Grid Rewards ~€550/yr per 15–20 kWh battery — [Solar Magazine NL, 26 Aug 2026](https://solarmagazine.nl/nieuws-zonne-energie/i44608/tibber-thuisbatterij-levert-550-euro-extra-op-door-openstellen-europese-intradaymarkt).

### Inferences
- In Germany, the legitimate monetisable quantity is the supplier's/aggregator's own forecast error: reducing reBAP exposure of a portfolio of PV+battery+EV households (volatile, iMSys-measured, increasingly on dynamic tariffs). That is a classic forecasting problem and a credible, low-regulatory-risk product for a software vendor, sold to the BKV (supplier or Direktvermarkter), not to households. Deliberate imbalance trading is not an available product.
- Because reBAP is anchored to the quarter-hour intraday price and carries a scarcity add-on, the economic value of better short-term load/PV forecasts for a residential portfolio is real but modest per customer; it scales with portfolio size, which again favours selling to large BKVs.

### Gaps
- The text of StromNZV §4 (balance-group duty) and the Standardbilanzkreisvertrag 2024 clauses on Bilanzkreistreue and sanctions were not readable (gesetze-im-internet returned 503 twice; 50Hertz PDF not fetched). Confirm: https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/stromnzv/__4.html and the 50Hertz Standardbilanzkreisvertrag PDF.

## Key question 7: Energy sharing (§42c EnWG), gemeinschaftliche Gebäudeversorgung (§42b), and whether energy communities buy optimisation software

### Takeaway
§42c EnWG (energy sharing over the public grid) entered into force on 23 Dec 2025 and DSOs must enable it within their own balancing area from 1 June 2026 (adjacent areas from June 2028); there is no grid-fee reduction, full supplier duties apply above 30 kW (100 kW in multi-party buildings), each site needs 15-minute metering, and legal commentators call it "wohl vorerst Nischenprodukt". Energy communities are therefore not a realistic 2026 buyer of optimisation software; the only plausible buyers are the billing/metering service providers that will run sharing for them.

### Cited Findings
- §42c EnWG introduced by Art. 1 Nr. 68 of the law of 18 Dec 2025, in force since 23 Dec 2025; DSOs must enable sharing within their balancing area from June 2026, adjacent areas in the same control zone from June 2028; distinct from §42b gemeinschaftliche Gebäudeversorgung (Solarpaket I, no public grid) — [Taylor Wessing, 26 Nov 2025](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/insights-and-events/insights/2025/11/energy-sharing-42-enwg); [logicenergy](https://www.logicenergy.de/neuigkeiten/energy-sharing-2026); [energiezukunft, 11 May 2026](https://www.energiezukunft.eu/buergerenergie/strom-teilen-schon-bald-moeglich-theoretisch).
- Mechanics (GÖRG, 13 Apr 2026): operator must be a natural person, partnership or private-law entity whose members are all end consumers or public bodies, non-commercial (e.g., cooperatives); consumers = households, municipal bodies, SMEs only; no capacity cap, but supplier duties (§§5, 40–42 EnWG) eased only for plants <=30 kW (<=100 kW in multi-party buildings); consumption at each site and plant output must be metered as 15-minute time series; no reduction of grid fees or levies; balancing-group management may be outsourced to a commercial service provider (§42c Abs. 5); no full-supply obligation, so participants need a residual supplier; authors: high entry barriers for volunteer groups, "wohl vorerst Nischenprodukt" — [GÖRG](https://www.goerg.de/de/aktuelles/veroeffentlichungen/13-04-2026/bald-strom-mit-nachbarn-teilen-ein-ausblick-auf-das-ab-1-juni-2026-geltende-energy-sharing-gemaess-ss-42c-enwg-chancen-und-grenzen).
- Bündnis Bürgerenergie: the rule is only a "Minimalvariante" of what practice needs — [energiezukunft](https://www.energiezukunft.eu/wirtschaft/enwg-novelle-im-bundestag-beschlossen). Smart-meter gaps and DSO allocation/billing capacity are cited as practical barriers — [energiezukunft, 11 May 2026](https://www.energiezukunft.eu/buergerenergie/strom-teilen-schon-bald-moeglich-theoretisch).
- Noerr commentary on the draft — [Noerr](https://www.noerr.com/de/insights/enwg-novelle-entwurf-zum-energy-sharing).

### Inferences
- With no grid-fee benefit, the economic surplus of energy sharing is the spread between the sharing price and the residual-supply price, split among a handful of participants; there is no budget for paid optimisation. Any software demand is for allocation/billing (15-minute allocation keys, MaKo), which is a market for metering/billing service providers (wettbewerbliche MSB, Stadtwerke IT houses), not for forecasting models.
- Bürgerenergie cooperatives are volunteer-run and explicitly non-commercial under §42c; they are not €5–50k software buyers.

### Gaps
- No count of energy communities or of §42c registrations after 1 June 2026 was found; AEE's background paper (referenced by energiezukunft) lists pilots but was not fetched.
- Status of §42b (Gebäudeversorgung) uptake in 2026: not researched beyond the legal distinction.

## Key question 9: Buyers who could pay €5k–€50k for a model, and who already has in-house teams; overall wedge judgment for a Munich/Portugal two-founder software team

### Takeaway
The large residential players (Enpal/Flexa, 1KOMMA5°, sonnen, Tibber, Octopus/Kraken, LichtBlick) build in-house and are becoming suppliers of infrastructure to others, not buyers of models; the realistic €5–50k buyers are second-tier BKVs that face new forecasting problems without data-science teams — regional suppliers and Stadtwerke selling dynamic tariffs, independent Direktvermarkter/BSPs onboarding small assets ahead of EEG 2027, metering/billing service providers, and possibly DSOs implementing Module 3. ### Inferences
- Closed segments for an outsider: household dispatch/HEMS (owned by inverter/HEMS vendors and integrators with device APIs), VPP trading for the big five fleets (in-house or JV), balancing-market access (BSP-owned, TSO PQ), energy sharing (no budget). The founders have no device access, no Bilanzkreis, no meter, and no customer base; every listed player has all four.
- Open-looking but thin: (1) quarter-hourly load/PV/EV forecasting and reBAP-exposure reduction for regional suppliers/Stadtwerke that now sell dynamic tariffs on iMSys data — a pure-data problem with a clear buyer (the BKV) and a measurable KPI (imbalance cost per MWh), fitting a €5–50k pilot; (2) small-asset Direktvermarktung onboarding ahead of 2027 for independent Direktvermarkter who lack residential forecasting (feed-in forecasts for sub-25 kW PV with 50% caps and battery behaviour); (3) Module-3-aware scheduling as a feature licensed to a HEMS vendor or regional supplier, low value per customer. All three require the partner to hand over data and to have at least thousands of iMSys customers; with 5.9% iMSys penetration, few second-tier suppliers do yet.
- Realism for a Munich + Portugal team without network or capital: the German customer set (Stadtwerke, regional suppliers, Direktvermarkter) buys through references, trade fairs (The smarter E / ees Europe, E-world), and procurement processes; a two-person foreign-ish team with no reference is at a strong disadvantage, and the incumbents (Enpal "Hyperion", Octopus Kraken, Tibber) are explicitly moving to sell the same stack to those second-tier buyers. The honest judgment from these sources: Germany's small-asset flexibility space is largely closed to a software-only entrant on the household/VPP side; a narrow B2B forecasting wedge toward BKVs exists in principle but is unproven and will need one anchor customer obtained through a personal introduction rather than inbound demand.
