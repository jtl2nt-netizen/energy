# Two wedges survive Europe's flexibility screen

**Yes, start — but narrower than the brief imagines, and upstream of where the anecdote points.** The evidence from ten research notes (roughly 300 dated 2025–2026 sources, with the gaps they could not close carried into the register below) says that a two-founder team with strong ML/optimisation skills, a Munich base, a Portuguese co-founder on the ground, no network, no proprietary data and no capital should not try to aggregate households, run a VPP, or become a trading party. Every household-facing route in Germany is closed by vertical integration (Enpal's 350-person tech team and 1KOMMA5°'s 1 GW Heartbeat fleet are now *selling* their stacks to the long tail) and by metering (5.9% smart-meter penetration at 30 June 2026), while per-asset value is tens of euros a year. What is open is a **model-vendor seam one layer below the parties that already hold dispatch and settlement rights**: (1) in Germany, shadow-mode forecasting and bidding sub-models (15-minute PV, aFRR-energy and intraday-spread forecasts, co-located PV+BESS dispatch signals) sold on a performance share to Munich-based battery optimisers and IPPs (Entrix, Kyon) and to mid-sized direct marketers whose margins are being compressed by a 16–40% fall in battery revenues and record negative-price hours; (2) in Iberia, 15-minute production forecasting and aFRR/mFRR bidding optimisation for renewable-plus-storage owners, with Portugal (99% smart meters, a fully 15-minute MIBEL since October 2025, regulation charges that doubled to 13 €/MWh in 2025, an E-REDES flexibility contract with no penalties, and a 2026 procurement code that lets public utilities trial software for free) as the design-partner market and Spain (600–800 zero/negative hours a year, a €255 million per half-year demand-response service, a capacity market created in September 2026) as the revenue market. Testing both costs roughly €5,000 a month of founder subsistence plus about $100 a month of weather data, needs no hardware and no licence, and can produce a public-data backtest in 30 days and a first paid diagnostic (€5–30k) within 90 days. The strong alternative, if interviews show cooperatives buy benchmark models, is quarter-hourly load/PV forecasting for second-tier balance-group managers (Thüga/Syneco in Munich, Südweststrom, Trianel). Abandon if, by day 90, no optimiser or direct marketer will share a month of realised dispatch data, if the public-data backtest cannot beat a rule-based dispatcher by more than the partner's likely fee, or if the only buyers found price the work at the €2–25k per year that commodity forecast vendors already charge. This is a consulting-to-niche-software business before it is anything else; the venture-scale outcomes in this market went to firms that took market risk or owned assets, and the notes found no public precedent of a two-person team licensing a stand-alone model to a German utility.

## 1. Executive verdict: start narrow, sell upstream, decide in 90 days

The brief's primary decision asks for one or two precise combinations of geography, market mechanism, customer, controllable asset and product. The two that survive the screen, with their lawful route to settlement, are set out in the table; the reasons they beat the alternatives are developed in sections 4 and 5.

| | Wedge 1 — Germany, "quant sub-supplier" | Wedge 2 — Iberia, "15-minute forecasting for renewables + storage" |
|---|---|---|
| Geography | Munich first (Entrix, Kyon, Thüga/Syneco, SWM are all in the city); then Hamburg/Vienna optimisers remotely | Portugal for design partners and references (co-founder on the ground); Spain for revenue through MIBEL commonality |
| Mechanism where money is made | aFRR capacity and energy, continuous intraday / IDA, 15-minute day-ahead, reBAP imbalance settlement, EEG market premium with curtailment at negative prices | OMIE 15-minute day-ahead and intraday, REN/REE aFRR/mFRR band and energy, 15-minute proportional imbalance settlement (desvios), E-REDES FIRMe local flexibility, Spanish SRAD and (2027) capacity market |
| Buyer and role | Head of Quant / Head of Trading at a BESS optimiser or mid-sized Direktvermarkter; Head of Asset Optimisation at an IPP | Asset-management / trading team at a renewable owner with co-located BESS; energy-sourcing manager at an indexed-offer retailer; Entrix/enspired Iberian teams needing local-rule expertise |
| Controllable asset | 1–100 MW BESS and co-located PV+BESS; PV portfolios of 500 MW–3 GW | Solar/wind plants with co-located BESS (≈1,250 MW of band enabled for balancing in Portugal at end-2025; PRR 500 MW cohort; 1,050 MVA auction); standalone BESS in Spain |
| Software deliverable | API-delivered 15-minute PV/wind nowcasts, aFRR-energy and ID-spread forecasts, co-location dispatch schedules; shadow-mode benchmark against the partner's realised P&L; model documentation for REMIT Art. 5a | 15-minute production forecasts and bid curves for aFRR/mFRR; imbalance-cost diagnostic on the client's own settlement data; FIRMe availability scheduling |

| Price form | Performance share of measured uplift (the enspired precedent) with a small fixed diagnostic fee | Paid diagnostic under Portugal's new direct-award limit (<€75,000), then success fee (the Bamboo precedent) |

The verdict rests on five findings that recur across the notes. First, money in European flexibility is paid to **licensed contractual roles** (balance-responsible party, balancing-service provider, direct marketer, supplier, congestion-service provider), never to the forecast as such; a software vendor's product is therefore an input to those roles' systems, and no source found imposed a registration on an analytics vendor in Germany or Portugal, though both notes flag this as an absence of evidence rather than a regulator statement ([buzer.de MsbG §49](https://www.buzer.de/s1.htm?a=49&g=MsbG&dorg=1); [SRS Legal on DL 15/2022](https://www.srslegal.pt/xms/files/Nota_Informativa_Novas_regras_de_organizacao_e_funcionamento_do_Sistema_Eletrico_Nacional.pdf)). Second, **residential flexibility value in Germany is small and captured by integrators**: HTW Berlin's 2026 simulation of 27 real households finds price-optimised battery control saves on average 233 €/a without PV but only 64 €/a with PV ([HTW poster 2026](https://solar.htw-berlin.de/wp-content/uploads/MEISSNER-2026-Poster-Einfluss-dynamischer-Stromtarife-und-zeitvariabler-Netzentgelte-auf-Haushalte-mit-PV-Batteriespeichersystem.pdf)), and Enpal, 1KOMMA5° and Octopus/Kraken build in-house and now sell their stacks outward ([pv magazine, 18 Jun 2026](https://www.pv-magazine.de/2026/06/18/enpal-stellt-seine-infrastruktur-anderen-marktteilnehmern-zur-verfuegung/); [1KOMMA5°, 20 May 2026](https://1komma5.com/en/press/press-releases/1komma5-hits-gigawatt-milestone-with-its-heartbeat-ai-virtual-power-plant)). Third, **utility-scale battery revenue is compressing and converging**, which moves optimiser differentiation from market access to forecast and bidding quality: the ISEA/RWTH index fell from ~309 k€/MW/yr (2024) to ~259 k€ (2025) and Q1 2026 annualised at ~132 k€ (−40% y/y) ([pv magazine, 9 Apr 2026](https://www.pv-magazine.de/2026/04/09/erloespotenziale-fuer-batteriespeicher-im-ersten-quartal-2026-tiefpunkt-im-februar-erholung-im-maerz/)), and asset owners already dual-source optimisers (Kyon chose both enspired and Entrix "to mitigate any risks") ([ESS News, 24 Oct 2025](https://www.ess-news.com/?p=7062)). Fourth, **the model is separable from integration and market access**: suena sells trading intelligence while VPP integrators handle asset communication ([pv magazine, 7 Jan 2024](https://www.pv-magazine.com/2024/01/07/german-startup-suena-secures-financing/)), which is the seam a two-person team can occupy. Fifth, **Iberia has sharper price signals, cleaner data and a thinner vendor field than Germany but a small Portuguese asset base**: the 15-minute chain is fully live (intraday March 2025, imbalance April 2025, day-ahead October 2025), regulation charges reached 13.06 €/MWh in 2025 against 7.99 in 2024, and enabled renewable/battery balancing band rose from ~375 MW to ~1,250 MW in a year ([ERSE Relatório Anual Mercados 2025](https://www.erse.pt/media/y0glwi30/relat%C3%B3rio_ce-2025_pt.pdf)), while Spain recorded 784 zero-or-negative hours in 2024 and 607 in the first half of 2025 ([El Periódico de la Energía](https://elperiodicodelaenergia.com/espana-registra-607-horas-de-precios-negativos-o-cero-hasta-junio-en-el-pool-electrico/)).

What not to pursue, and why, in one line each: a **German residential VPP or HEMS optimiser** (closed by integrators, inverter APIs and the smart-meter bottleneck; value tens of euros per asset-year); **Dutch household imbalance trading** (returns measured at €74–178 per 10 kW per month and falling, "regeltoestand 2" at ~40% of 2026 against a <10% obligation, TenneT contemplating a 15-minute price delay, supplier lock-in); **V2G** (every live offer is an OEM–utility bundle with a 720 €/yr availability cap where optimiser quality barely moves the outcome); **energy communities as direct buyers** (no budget anywhere: §42c in Germany is "wohl vorerst Nischenprodukt", Portuguese ACCs are settled automatically by E-REDES with proportional coefficients, Austrian EEG gains are low four figures per community); **principal BESS optimisation** (needs capital, floor guarantees and 24/7 desks — Entrix raised €43m, terralayr €192m, Flexpower sold to Citadel). Each is argued with sources in section 4.

Three caveats bound the verdict. Every researcher ran out of web-search budget part-way and could not reach several primary pages (ENTSO-E terms, TenneT and Elia pages behind Cloudflare, ACER REMIT open letters, EPEX fee schedules, Portuguese desvios price series, the status of Portugal's September 2026 storage auction); these are listed in the register rather than filled from memory. No note found a public case of a small independent team licensing a stand-alone forecasting model to a German utility or aggregator in 2025–2026; the closest precedents are founder-led Datasphere Analytics selling price forecasts to a Stadtwerke association through a 6–12-month parallel run ([ZfK, 12 Aug 2026](https://www.zfk.de/digitalisierung/ki/die-ki-ist-einfach-ein-marktanalyst)) and enspired reaching live client trading six months after founding with performance-based pricing ([Mission Innovation, Dec 2020](https://mission-innovation.net/champions-corner-jurgen-mayerhofer-revolutionizes-power-trading/)). And the quantitative anchors for buyer value (what a Stadtwerk pays in imbalance cost; what an optimiser pays for a forecast feed) are estimates or vendor figures, not published contracts.

Two features of the chain deserve emphasis because they shape the wedges. In Germany, the real-time AEP-Schätzer published 30 minutes after each quarter-hour had a mean absolute error of only 3.07 €/MWh against the final reBAP in Q1 2026 ([netztransparenz AEP-Schätzer](https://www.netztransparenz.de/de-de/Regelenergie/Ausgleichsenergiepreis/AEP-Sch%C3%A4tzer)), so "predicting the imbalance price after the fact" has no value; the money is in forecasting the sign and size of the system balance before gate closure and in reducing one's own quarter-hourly error now that day-ahead, intraday and imbalance all run on one 15-minute grid since 1 October 2025 ([APG, 1 Oct 2025](https://markt.apg.at/en/news-press/sdac-go-live-of-15-minute-market-time-unit-in-day-ahead-market-coupling-successful-as-of-september-30-2025/)). In Iberia, imbalance cost is allocated pro rata to all deviating agents from the actual reserve energy used, so the value of a better forecast is bounded by the regulation charge (~13 €/MWh of consumption in Portugal) rather than by a scarcity-priced penalty; this makes utility-scale producers with large absolute deviations, not small retailers, the natural first buyer.

The cashflow diagram for Wedge 1, in words: the asset owner (IPP or C&I) contracts an optimiser or direct marketer, which holds the balancing group, the TSO prequalification and the exchange membership and takes a fee or revenue share; the startup contracts with the optimiser, receives read-only telemetry and realised dispatch and settlement data under an Art. 28 GDPR processor agreement where personal data is involved, returns forecasts and dispatch signals through an API, and is paid a fixed diagnostic fee plus a share of measured uplift against the optimiser's own baseline; market risk, imbalance risk and REMIT Art. 5a documentation duties stay with the optimiser, which "must know and be able to show documentation of the underlying logic" of any vendor algorithm it uses ([Nord Pool Market Surveillance, Dec 2024](https://nordpoolgroup.com/48cc74/globalassets/download-center/market-surveillance/market-surveillance-newsletter---december-2024.pdf)). For Wedge 2, replace the optimiser with the renewable owner's trading team or its Iberian representative, and the settlement point with the owner's market-agent code at OMIE/REN.

Three lessons from the matrix override the brief's original geographic hypotheses. Deep negative prices do not make a market: Bulgaria, Hungary and Poland have the most violent wholesale prices in the set and the least retail pass-through, so the only paying customers there are utility-scale battery owners already served by Entrix-class optimisers. The markets where small-asset money actually flows in 2026 (Italy's CER premium, the Netherlands' household imbalance trade, Austria's community grid-fee discount, Britain's DSO flexibility) are either language-locked, vertically integrated, thin per asset, or non-portable. And the two markets where the founders have presence — Germany and Portugal/Spain — are also the two where the rules, data and buyers line up for a model vendor, provided the vendor sells upstream. Italy would be the strongest "first market" for a team with Italian-language go-to-market; for this team it is a second market at best.

Sensitivity of the ranking. W1 and W2 are one point apart, which is within scoring noise; they should be treated as a pair, not a ranking, and in practice they share customers (Entrix markets in Spain and Portugal and raised €43m partly for Iberian expansion; enspired entered Spain with Nexus Energía in 2025; Kyon is owned by TotalEnergies, whose Portuguese solar farms already switched forecasting vendor) ([tech.eu, 25 Mar 2026](https://tech.eu/2026/03/25/entrix-raises-eur43m-as-contracted-battery-storage-portfolio-reaches-3gw/); [Techleap](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/feed/enspired-secures-25-5m-expands-to-spain); [Jungle AI](https://www.jungle.ai/blog-posts/jungle-expands-power-forecasting-service-to-totalenergies)). Doubling the weight on "close first deal" and "geography fit" keeps W1 first and lifts W3 above W2, because Thüga/Syneco sits in Munich; doubling the weight on "net incremental value" and "competition" puts W2 first and drops W3 to fifth. No plausible re-weighting lifts W5–W8 into the top three. The honest reading is that the founders' two locations select the two top wedges, and the evidence does not say which of the two will produce the first invoice.

Why W1 ranks where it does: buyer pain is documented (revenue compression, "forecast quality is becoming more important", higher service fees for volatility risk ([ZfK, 20 Jan 2025](https://www.zfk.de/unternehmen/vertrieb/ranking-die-zwanzig-groessten-direktvermarkter-2025))), access is clean (no licence, partner holds everything), data for a backtest is public, and three buyers are in Munich. It loses points on competition (Dexter has 53 experts, Enpal 350 engineers, the top-seven marketers run quant desks) and on defensibility (an accuracy edge is copyable within a product cycle). Why W2 ranks equally: the structural signals are stronger (regulation charges doubled, 1,250 MW of enabled band, a 1,050 MVA auction, Spanish 2-h batteries at 477 k€/MW/yr in July 2026), the vendor field is thinner, and the co-founder gives Portuguese-language access to ERSE, REN, E-REDES and EDP; it loses on data (the Portuguese desvios price series could not be found; OMIE/ESIOS licences unverified) and on the small Portuguese asset base. Why W3 is the alternative, not the lead: the value is real only at ≥1 TWh of load (an order-of-magnitude estimate puts a 10–20% error reduction at 150–400 k€/yr for a 1 TWh supplier but 15–40 k€/yr for a 100 GWh one, on unsourced error assumptions), the median Stadtwerk is a town of 20–50k inhabitants, and the documented sales motion is a 6–12-month parallel run against both the buyer's own forecasts and the incumbent vendor ([EY/BDEW Stadtwerkestudie 2025](https://www.bdew.de/media/original_images/2025/06/02/stadtwerkestudie-2025-ey-und-bdew_150dpi.pdf); [ZfK, 12 Aug 2026](https://www.zfk.de/digitalisierung/ki/die-ki-ist-einfach-ein-marktanalyst)).

The five attractive-looking alternatives are rejected on the evidence, not on taste.

**Residential VPP in Germany.** The three largest residential players build in-house and are now vendors of their stacks: Enpal's Energy-Tech unit (Flexa, Enpal.One, Enpal Energy, Metrify) will offer tariff, VPP and its "Hyperion" software to households without Enpal hardware and to "hardware makers, large-storage operators and commercial customers" ([pv magazine, 18 Jun 2026](https://www.pv-magazine.de/2026/06/18/enpal-stellt-seine-infrastruktur-anderen-marktteilnehmern-zur-verfuegung/)); 1KOMMA5° opened Heartbeat AI to utilities, grid operators and installers with >€100m to invest in the software business ([1KOMMA5°, 20 May 2026](https://1komma5.com/en/press/press-releases/1komma5-hits-gigawatt-milestone-with-its-heartbeat-ai-virtual-power-plant)). Only 5.9% of meters had an iMSys at 30 June 2026, so the settled-on-spot universe is low single-digit millions at most ([BNetzA, 21 Sep 2026](https://www.bundesnetzagentur.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/DE/2026/20260921_Rollout.html)); Verivox finds dynamic tariffs "fast immer teurer als die günstigsten Festpreistarife" for a non-shifting household ([Solarserver, 16 Jul 2026](https://www.solarserver.de/2026/07/16/verivox-dynamische-stromtarife-teurer-als-festpreistarife/)); a 10 kWh/5 kW battery "cannot recoup its purchase cost" on a dynamic tariff alone in the Neon/Naturstrom simulation ([Neon, 27 Oct 2025](https://neon.energy/Neon-Dynamischer-Stromtarif.pdf)); published household payments are €100–200/yr-scale (Tibber Grid Rewards up to €150/yr for EVs; sonnen's historical €119/yr) ([electrive, 11 Sep 2026](https://www.electrive.net/2026/09/11/tibber-startet-grid-rewards/)). The only documented case of a residential incumbent outsourcing trading intelligence is Enpal's majority-owned Flexa JV with an already-funded optimiser, not a vendor contract ([pv magazine, 5 Nov 2024](https://www.pv-magazine.de/2024/11/05/enpal-meldet-start-seines-virtuellen-kraftwerks/)).

**Dutch household imbalance.** It is real and paid, but per-battery returns are compressing on three independent-ish sources (Mijnbatterij's measurement on >400 batteries; Zonneplan's own statement that PICASSO flattens peaks faster; a "large aggregator" warning of saturation) ([Thuisbatterijnederland](https://thuisbatterijnederland.nl/onbalansmarkt-thuisbatterij/); [Duurzaam Thuis Lab](https://duurzaamthuislab.nl/posts/zonneplan-thuisbatterij-review-2026/); [VOLL Energie](https://voll-energie.nl/bsp-en-afrr-bij-een-thuisbatterij-wat-betekent-dat-eigenlijk/)), the value is captured by retailer-BRPs that lock customers into their supply contract, TenneT treats synchronised battery reactions to its balance delta as a stability problem, and a ~€0.11/kWh double energy tax on grid-charged cycles is unresolved from 2027. Tibber itself avoids the Dutch imbalance market as "a small market whose returns fall as more parties join" ([Solar Magazine NL, 26 Aug 2026](https://solarmagazine.nl/nieuws-zonne-energie/i44608/tibber-thuisbatterij-levert-550-euro-extra-op-door-openstellen-europese-intradaymarkt)). The forecasting version of this niche (W6) is credible but Dexter already sells it and every BRP named runs its own steering stack.

**V2G.** Every live product is an OEM–utility bundle: BMW iX3 + E.ON with a 0.24 €/h plug-in bonus capped at 720 €/yr plus 0.40 €/kWh exported, orderable from February 2026 ([IAA Mobility, 13 Feb 2026](https://www.iaa-mobility.com/en/newsroom/news/future-technology/bmw-eon-officially-launch-bidirectional-v2g-offering-germany)); VW/Elli with the same cap for Q4 2026; Octopus Power Pack in the UK limited to BYD Dolphin + Zaptec. The revenue is an availability payment, so optimiser quality barely moves it; no open third-party V2G dispatch API was found; MiSpeL metering rules were still "in progress" in March 2026 ([Moddemann, 16 Mar 2026](https://www.moddemann.de/artikel/bidirektionales-laden-2026/)). V1G is the lawful, repeatable alternative and is worth only ~164 €/yr per car on a German dynamic tariff, rising to ~440 €/yr when §14a Modules 1+3 are stacked — a value dominated by tariff enrolment, not by the optimiser ([Neon, 27 Oct 2025](https://neon.energy/Neon-Dynamischer-Stromtarif.pdf)).

**Energy communities as direct buyers.** No note found any community operator in any country paying a third party for forecasting or optimisation. Germany's §42c gives no grid-fee reduction, requires 15-minute metering at every site and non-commercial operators ([GÖRG](https://www.goerg.de/de/aktuelles/veroeffentlichungen/13-04-2026/bald-strom-mit-nachbarn-teilen-ein-ausblick-auf-das-ab-1-juni-2026-geltende-energy-sharing-gemaess-ss-42c-enwg-chancen-und-grenzen)); Portuguese ACCs share "quase exclusivamente" by proportional coefficients that E-REDES applies automatically, with dynamic sharing in a single pilot ([ERSE Relatório do Autoconsumo 2024](https://www.erse.pt/media/fkwdrz13/relat%C3%B3rio-sobre-o-autoconsumo.pdf)); Austrian EEG members gain at most 2.47 ct/kWh plus a 0.82 ct/kWh tax exemption, which for a 100-member community is low four figures a year; Italian CER consumer members earn ~15–50 €/yr by Enpal's own promotional estimate ([Enpal CER guide](https://www.enpal.com/it/fotovoltaico/guida-completa-comunita-energetiche-rinnovabili)). The buyers, if any, are the platforms (Cleanwatts with a €150m DWS plan, neoom, Exnaton, eFriends), and they are more likely to build than buy (Cleanwatts has Kiplo; Smartwatt spun off Enlitia).

**Principal BESS optimisation.** The German optimiser market is now capital-intensive and consolidating around trading houses and asset owners: Flexpower sold 100% to Citadel ([pv magazine, 13 Oct 2025](https://www.pv-magazine.de/2025/10/13/investor-citadel-uebernimmt-flexpower/)); terralayr raised €192m and owns assets ([Evertiq, 27 Jan 2026](https://evertiq.com/news/2026-01-27-terralayr-announces-192-million-equity-raise)); enspired offers tolling and floor-with-cap structures that require a balance sheet. A two-person team cannot offer floors, 24/7 desks or prequalified pools; it can only supply the model layer to those who do.

The notes also surface three pockets the brief would call counterintuitive, which are recorded here because they feed the 6–12-month branches rather than the first wedge: the Italian South/islands zonal arbitrage before the PUN sunset (106 zero-price hours in Q2 2026 while retail still sees PUN-equivalent prices); Romania's >800 MW of prosumer batteries with a 1 MW aggregation floor and a first demand-only mFRR activation on 1 September 2026 but no VPP operator; and Czech sharing groups losing up to 50% of shareable energy to static allocation keys until the EDC's dynamic functions land ([EC Unlock your power – Czechia](https://energy.ec.europa.eu/topics/markets-and-consumers/energy-consumers-and-prosumers/unlock-your-power/unlock-your-power-czechia_en)).

## 5. Deep dives: what each top wedge needs, earns and runs into

### Wedge 1 — Germany: forecast and bidding sub-models for optimisers and mid-sized direct marketers

*Regulation.* The vendor delivers forecasts, bid curves and dispatch schedules into the client's systems; the client remains the Bilanzkreisverantwortlicher, the balancing-service provider under the TSO framework agreement and the REMIT market participant. The German supplier notification (§5 EnWG) attaches to supplying household customers, not to analytics ([BNetzA Lieferantenanzeige FAQ](https://bundesnetzagentur.de/DE/Fachthemen/ElektrizitaetundGas/HandelundVertrieb/Lieferantenanzeige/start.html)); meter data reaches the vendor as an Art. 28 GDPR processor for an entitled party under MsbG §49(3) ([buzer.de](https://www.buzer.de/s1.htm?a=49&g=MsbG&dorg=1)); REMIT II Art. 5a puts algorithmic-trading duties on the market participant, and ACER's guidance (relayed by Nord Pool) says those duties cover "stand-alone vendor algorithms by third parties", so the client will demand model documentation, change logs, a kill switch and a vendor questionnaire ([Nord Pool, Dec 2024](https://nordpoolgroup.com/48cc74/globalassets/download-center/market-surveillance/market-surveillance-newsletter---december-2024.pdf)). The line not to cross without counsel: if the vendor's software is given credentials to submit orders to EPEX or regelleistung.net without human intervention, the vendor may be "professionally arranging or executing transactions" (a PPAET) — the notes found no source settling this. NIS2 (in force in Germany since 6 December 2025) applies only from 50 staff or €10m turnover and reaches a small vendor through client supply-chain clauses ([usd.de](https://www.usd.de/nis2umsucg-offiziell-in-kraft/)). Simplest lawful entry: advisory-only delivery under a Dienstvertrag with liability capped at fees and a €1m Vermögensschadenhaftpflicht (indicatively ~€50/month) ([Hiscox](https://www.hiscox.de/geschaeftskunden/it-haftpflichtversicherung)).

*Data.* Public: SMARD day-ahead (CC BY 4.0, attribution "Bundesnetzagentur | SMARD.de"), netztransparenz reBAP (final by the 20th working day of the following month), AEP-Schätzer (T+30 min) and ID-AEP (D+1), regelleistung.net tender results, MaStR plant registry (3.2 GB daily XML, Datenlizenz Deutschland 2.0), Open-Meteo Previous Runs for fixed-lead-time weather since January 2024 at the Professional tier ($99/month per Open-Meteo's newsletter, to be verified) ([SMARD](https://www.smard.de/home/datennutzung); [Netztransparenz Erläuterungen](https://www.netztransparenz.de/xspproxy/api/staticfiles/ntp-relaunch/dokumente/regelenergie/regelenergie%20(%C3%BCs)/rl-erlaeuterungen_ntp_20230710.pdf); [Open-Meteo Previous Runs](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/previous-runs-api)). Do not use Energy-Charts `/price` for DE-LU in a product: the API marks most price zones "private and internal use" ([Energy-Charts openapi](https://api.energy-charts.info/openapi.json)). Partner-only: continuous-intraday execution and slippage (EPEX order-book data is behind paid Market Data Services whose fee pages returned 403), per-asset SoC telemetry, availability, degradation, prequalification constraints, and the optimiser's realised P&L; Modo's German index substitutes a "centred 2-hour rolling mean" for ID3 execution and a flat 0.80 calibration factor because "Germany has no public per-asset battery data" ([Modo methodology](https://modoenergy.com/benchmarking/methodology/germany)).

*Economics (per 10 MW/20 MWh asset, gross to the asset, 2026 conditions).* Conservative ~150 k€/MW/yr (Environ 12-month average ~155 k€ to July 2026; December 2025 monthly low 5.7 k€/MW), base ~200 k€/MW/yr (ISEA March 2026 annualised; ISEA multi-use benchmark 195–214 k€), upside ~260 k€/MW/yr (ISEA 2025 cross-market) ([photovoltaik.sh, 30 Aug 2026](https://www.photovoltaik.sh/news/erloespotenziale-und-wirtschaftlichkeit-von-netzgekoppelten-grossbatteriespeichern-in-deutschland-2026); [pv magazine, 23 Jan 2026](https://www.pv-magazine.de/2026/01/23/erloespotenziale-fuer-stationaere-batteriespeicher-in-deutschland-sind-2025-zurueckgegangen/)). The sales argument is the gap between benchmark and realised (195–214 k€ vs ~155 k€), but the vendor's capturable slice is small per asset: a 5% uplift on a 10 MW asset at base is ~100 k€/yr, and at a 20–30% share the vendor earns ~20–30 k€/yr per 10 MW — a six-figure contract needs a 50–100 MW portfolio or a whole-portfolio feed to an optimiser. Within the stack, FCR has collapsed (1.35 GW prequalified vs 0.53–0.56 GW demand; realised FCR contribution −90%+ in 2025), aFRR capacity is the anchor (2-h system >146 k€/MW/yr in 2025) and positive aFRR delivered ~80% of storage revenue in a co-location backtest ([EERA Battery Beats](https://www.eera-consulting.de/battery-beats/erloese-co-location-bess-2025-oktober)), which tells the vendor where its first model should be: aFRR capacity-price and energy-activation forecasting, then ID spreads, then 15-minute DA. Academic MPC with price forecasts captures 50–90% of perfect-foresight arbitrage, >80% for ≥2 h batteries ([arXiv 2108.06000](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.06000)), so the claimable improvement over a competent incumbent is in the 5–15% range, and ranking forecasters by battery-trading profit is gameable, so the test must report proper scoring rules alongside realised value ([arXiv 2604.19580](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19580)). For the direct-marketer variant, at the unverified 0.1–0.4 ct/kWh fee a 1 GW PV portfolio earns the marketer ~1–4 M€/yr gross from which it covers its EEG balancing-group imbalance, so forecast error is the main margin lever and a 10% error reduction is worth low-to-mid six figures to a mid-sized marketer — a hypothesis to test in interviews, not a number to quote.

*Competitors.* Entrix (3 GW contracted, €43m, Munich), enspired (4.3+ GWh, Vienna), Flexpower (Citadel), suena (€8m Series A led by Eneco Ventures), terralayr; the top direct marketers (Quadra 10,100 MW, EnBW 9,900, Next Kraftwerke 8,020, Statkraft 6,800) run their own forecasting; third-party forecasters emsys, enercast, Ubimet, ENFOR, Solcast price at €2–25k/yr per portfolio with no published tariffs ([KI-Syndikat](https://www.ki-syndikat.de/usecases/energie/08-erneuerbare-einspeise-prognose/)); Dexter (€23m) and Amperon ($30m) sell forecasts and trading signals to traders. The vendor competes with the optimiser's in-house quant team, not with the forecasters, and wins only where the optimiser is short of modelling capacity for a new market (Iberia, Poland), a new product (15-minute DA, Momentanreserve at 8–17 k€/MW/yr, 2030–33 regional dynamic grid fees) or a new asset type (co-location restricted to own-PV charging).

*Named candidate buyers and roles.* Entrix — Head of Quant/Trading (Munich; Iberian expansion); Kyon Energy — Head of Asset Optimisation (Munich; 309 MW realised, 1.2 GW under construction; in-house optimisation; BGH BKZ litigant, so site-level BKZ and flexible-connection modelling is a bounded consulting problem); suena — CTO/Head of Trading (Hamburg; targets municipal utilities); enspired — Head of Quant (Vienna; Spain/Portugal "future"); FlexPower — Head of Trading (1,400 assets); Stadtwerke München — Leiter Handel/Direktvermarktung (~2,400 MW book, −9% in 2024, third in PV among municipals); Trianel — Leiter Portfoliomanagement (2,900 MW direct marketing, Batteriepark Waltrop, says missing grid-connection agreements hold back batteries); MVV — Head of Trading (5,800 MW, first aFRR from onshore wind); Pure Energy, Gewi, Ane Energie, Sachsenenergie, in.power — Leiter Handel (500 MW–5 GW books without 24/7 quant teams); Esforin — Head of Flexibility Marketing (2.5 GW, 850 MW batteries) ([ZfK rankings 2025/2026](https://www.zfk.de/unternehmen/direktvermarktung/direktvermarktung-2025-die-spitze-rueckt-zusammen); [B2B note sources](https://entrixenergy.com/)).

*Costs and bottlenecks.* Cash cost of the test is laptop compute, ~$100/month weather data and founder time; the bottleneck is access to one month of realised dispatch and settlement data from one optimiser, which no public source substitutes. Second bottleneck: the 6–12-month parallel-run norm before a buyer switches or dual-sources. Third: the absence of a public playbook — no German press case of a two-person model shop landing an optimiser contract was found, so the first conversation has to be earned with a published backtest rather than a reference.

### Wedge 2 — Iberia: 15-minute forecasting and system-services bidding for renewables with storage, Portugal first

*Regulation.* DL 15/2022 makes retail supply and independent aggregation DGEG registration activities (aggregation of last resort needs a licence) and lets registered retailers aggregate after notifying DGEG; ERSE's register (updated 17 July 2026) lists market agents (ADM), aggregators (AGR) and retailers (COM) as separate categories ([SRS Legal](https://www.srslegal.pt/xms/files/Nota_Informativa_Novas_regras_de_organizacao_e_funcionamento_do_Sistema_Eletrico_Nacional.pdf); [ERSE registo](https://www.erse.pt/media/radffdkh/cria_atualizado.pdf)). No registration for a software vendor was found; DL 130/2026 (June 2026) amends the SEN regime and was not read in full, so the 2022 aggregator rules must be re-checked against it. The MPGGS revision (Diretiva 9/2025, after Consulta Pública 127) standardised aFRR, introduced a daily mFRR capacity product in 15-minute periods and operationalised aggregation of assets below 1 MW with an "uncorrected" imbalance model that EDP contested ([ERSE 2025](https://www.erse.pt/media/y0glwi30/relat%C3%B3rio_ce-2025_pt.pdf); [EDP response to CP 127](https://www.erse.pt/media/frof5ma2/edp-sa.pdf)). A draft despacho would require grid-forming operation and synthetic inertia for new BESS ≥500 kW, shifting value toward system services where bidding quality matters ([Ayming, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.ayming.pt/novidades/artigos/leilao-de-baterias-em-portugal-1-050-mva-para-armazenamento-de-energia/)). Portugal's new procurement code (DL 177/2026, in force 1 October 2026) raises direct award for services to <€75,000, allows free temporary provision of IT systems for technical evaluation (Art. 35.º-C), lets public entities reserve procedures for certified startups (Art. 54.º-A) and accepts spontaneous technical studies (Art. 35.º-B) — unusually favourable mechanics for a first paid diagnostic with a municipal or state-linked buyer ([PLMJ, Sept 2026](https://www.plmj.com/xms/files/03_Novidades_legislativas/2026/NI_CCP_2026.pdf)). In Spain, RD 88/2026 defines the independent aggregator but its provisions take effect only once CNMC formats and REE procedures exist, none of which had been published by September 2026 ([BOE RD 88/2026](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2026-3212); [MIAF](https://www.miaf.es/post/agregacion-demanda-analisis-flexibilidad-empresarial)); the lawful route in 2026 is therefore through a retailer or a producer's representative.

*Data.* OMIE publishes day-ahead and intraday prices, programmes, curves and bids for the last six years (legal notice unreachable, so reuse terms are unverified); ESIOS exposes indicators at five-minute to yearly groupings behind a personal token; REN's Data Hub API offers daily and monthly aggregates and explicitly excludes forecasts, imbalances and reserves; E-REDES publishes 70 datasets under CC BY 4.0 including hourly consumption by postal code, EV-mobility consumption, substation load diagrams and ACC counts ([OMIE file list](https://www.omie.es/en/file-access-list); [ESIOS API](https://api.esios.ree.es/); [REN API](https://datahub.ren.pt/en/api-instructions/); [E-REDES catalogue](https://e-redes.opendatasoft.com/api/explore/v2.1/catalog/datasets?limit=100)). The Portuguese desvios price series and secondary-band/mFRR capacity prices were not retrieved and must come from REN's settlement pages or the client. Third-party access to 15-minute meter data with consumer consent runs through the E-REDES Balcão Digital under RSRI Reg. 817/2023 and EU Reg. 2023/1162 ([ERSE Redes Inteligentes](https://www.erse.pt/atividade/regulamentos-eletricidade/redes-inteligentes/)), which is better than Germany's position but irrelevant to utility-scale plants. A data-quality hazard for any UPAC/ACC product: of 29,161 UPAC >4 kW only 22% deliver reliable production metering ([ERSE Relatório do Autoconsumo 2024](https://www.erse.pt/media/fkwdrz13/relat%C3%B3rio-sobre-o-autoconsumo.pdf)).

*Economics.* No Portuguese BESS revenue benchmark exists; the Spanish benchmarks are the anchor: Modo validates its Spanish day-ahead sub-line against Iberian TBx benchmarks of ~€80k/MW (2024) and ~€130k/MW (2025) ([Modo ES](https://modoenergy.com/research/es-bess-me-benchmark-revenue-stack)), Clean Horizon put Spanish 2-h batteries at 477 k€/MW/yr and 4-h at 650 k€/MW/yr in July 2026 on day-ahead, intraday and mFRR spreads ([ESS News, 2 Sep 2026](https://www.ess-news.com/2026/09/02/battery-revenues-surge-in-spain-and-poland-as-europe-sees-mixed-results/)), and a University of Seville study warns that 30 GWh of storage would cut the average daily peak–trough differential from €70.68 to €32.56/MWh and that beyond 32 GWh revenues no longer cover variable costs ([ESS News, 20 Jan 2026](https://www.ess-news.com/2026/01/20/battery-storage-in-spain-becomes-unviable-beyond-32-gwh-of-cumulative-capacity-study-finds/)). Conservative/base/upside for a 50 MW co-located PT plant: treat the 2024 Iberian TBx (~80 k€/MW/yr) as conservative, the 2025 figure (~130 k€/MW/yr) as base and the July 2026 Spanish spike as upside that will not persist; a 5–10% forecasting and bidding uplift on the base is 0.3–0.65 M€/yr for 50 MW, of which a success fee of 20–30% is 65–195 k€/yr — a single mid-sized Iberian plant is worth more to the vendor than ten Stadtwerke. For retailers, the bound is the regulation charge (~13 €/MWh in Portugal) on a few hundred GWh for the small ones, i.e. a few k€/month at best, which is why retailers are the second product, not the first. SRAD availability in Spain (42.62–65 €/MW·h, 2,279–4,371 hours a year) is rich relative to Germany but entered via large consumers and retailers ([srad.energy](https://srad.energy/)).

*Competitors.* Ignis Energia (first authorised independent aggregator in Portugal, February 2025; >8 GW represented in Iberia) and Axpo bundle forecasting with representation ([Jornal Económico, 5 Mar 2025](https://jornaleconomico.sapo.pt/noticias/ignis-energia-e-o-primeiro-agregador-independente-autorizado-a-participar-em-servicos-de-sistema-em-portugal/)); Jungle AI (Lisbon team, EDP Ventures-backed) replaced TotalEnergies' forecasting provider for its Alentejo solar farms after a three-month trial on accuracy alone, which both proves the buyer exists and names the incumbent to beat ([Jungle AI](https://www.jungle.ai/blog-posts/jungle-expands-power-forecasting-service-to-totalenergies)); Smartwatt's spin-off Enlitia sells renewables forecasting; Bamboo Energy (Barcelona, IREC spin-off, >€5.8m funding) sells forecasting-and-bidding SaaS to retailers with success-fee pricing and claims customers in Portugal ([El Español, 31 Jul 2025](https://www.elespanol.com/invertia/disruptores/ecosistema-startup/startups/20250731/lustro-bamboo-energy-luchando-apagones-historia-exito-startup-adelantada-tiempo/1003743867037_0.html)); Entrix and enspired are arriving with German-market tooling and will need local-rule expertise, which makes them potential customers for Wedge 2 as much as competitors.

*Named candidate buyers and roles.* Renewable owners with market-regime plants and the PRR BESS cohort: EDP (asset management / EDP Innovation lists "Storage & flexibility" as a domain), Galp (73 MW/147 MWh grid-forming BESS in Iberia; largest SRAD provider in Spain), Greenvolt, Finerge, Voltalia, Iberdrola PT, TotalEnergies REN Portugal, Capwatt; Infraventus (merchant 12 MVA/24 MWh Casal da Corticeira); representation houses Ignis and Axpo (Head of Portfolio/Trading); indexed-offer retailers Goldenergy, Luzboa, Coopérnico, Muon, Ágoraluz, MEO Energia, ENI Plenitude (energy-sourcing manager); E-REDES (FIRMe programme lead) and REN (system-operation data teams) as pilot partners rather than payers; in Spain, mid-sized retailers and SRAD providers (Fortia, MET, Energya VM, Axpo Iberia, Naturgy) and the Entra coalition as a channel ([ERSE Síntese Mensal Jan 2026](https://www.erse.pt/media/4t3a02p0/202601_ele_rel_ml.pdf); [EDP Innovation](https://www.edp.com/en/innovation)).

*Costs and bottlenecks.* The Portuguese co-founder's presence makes ERSE consultations, REN/E-REDES pilot conversations and Portuguese-language sales to the retailer tail cheap; the bottlenecks are the small asset base (≈120 MW BESS mid-2025 with 180 MW BigBATT due 2026 and ~500 MW of PRR co-located projects obliged to offer system services), PRR deadlines (end-2026) that push promoters toward hardware spend rather than software, and EDP's 59% retail share and in-house capability. Whether the 14 September 2026 auction launched and what it cleared could not be verified; that single fact decides whether a 2027 wave of BESS owners needing aFRR/mFRR tools exists.

### The alternative — Germany: quarter-hourly forecasting for second-tier balance-group managers

If interviews reveal that cooperatives or regional suppliers buy benchmark models, this becomes the lead. The buyer concentrates forecast error: Trianel runs >200 balancing groups and daily short-term forecasts for 53 Stadtwerke shareholders and 138 TWh of traded power; Syneco (Thüga's trading house, Munich) sells "Bottom-up-Prognosen" that "reduce balancing energy costs" to ~100 partner utilities; Südweststrom manages 24/7 balancing groups for 62 shareholders and energy data for >60 grid operators ([Trianel](https://www.trianel.com/produkte/liefer-und-bilanzkreismanagement); [Syneco](https://www.syneco.net/); [Südweststrom](https://www.suedweststrom.de/)). The product is asymmetric-loss quarter-hourly load and PV forecasting for PV-heavy or heat-pump-heavy balancing groups, benchmarked on a subset with a shared-savings fee. Three facts keep it the alternative: the EY/BDEW survey shows Stadtwerke boards rank IT security, process optimisation and staff above AI pilots, with 61% of utilities "still experimenting" with AI per a PwC figure relayed by a vendor blog ([EY/BDEW 2025](https://www.bdew.de/media/original_images/2025/06/02/stadtwerkestudie-2025-ey-und-bdew_150dpi.pdf); [innobu, 23 Jul 2026](https://www.innobu.com/de/artikel/ki-bilanzkreismanagement-ausgleichsenergie-2026.html)); the cooperatives call forecasting core IP ("in-house AI tools"); and the one quantified comparison — a Fraunhofer IPA model showing 7.95–16.96% lower procurement costs than "a commercial forecast service" — comes from a vendor blog and cannot be verified. Procurement is below the EU sector threshold (€432,000 net from 1 January 2026), so a 20–80 k€ pilot can be awarded directly by a Leiter Energiewirtschaft ([IHK](https://www.ihk.de/bodensee-oberschwaben/recht/vertraege-schliessen/oeffentliche-ausschreibung-vergabe/neue-schwellenwerte-europaweite-ausschreibungen-5989678)).

| Tooling | `pyomo` + `highspy`, `lightgbm`, `statsforecast`, `epftoolbox` for LEAR/DNN price baselines ([arXiv 2008.08004](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08004)), `openmeteo-requests` + `requests-cache`, direct CSV for SMARD/netztransparenz | same | same |

Two protocol rules are non-negotiable because they are where a buyer's quant will attack. First, weather features must be fixed-lead-time forecasts as issued: ECMWF Open Data keeps only the last 12 runs and DWD's retention is undocumented, so historical issue-time forecasts come from Open-Meteo's Previous Runs API (most models from January 2024) — and for 2022–2023 only the stitched Historical Forecast API exists, which flatters short-lead accuracy and must be disclosed ([ECMWF Open Data](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/forecasts/datasets/open-data); [Open-Meteo Historical Forecast](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-forecast-api)). Second, the German day-ahead series changes resolution on 1 October 2025, so every result must be reported for the hourly and the 15-minute regime separately; a pan-European benchmark found no systematic deterioration after the switch, which is the baseline a founder's model must at least match ([IAEE abstract 487](https://app.iaee.org/abstract/abstract-487.pdf)). The Iberian variants depend on one gap: the Portuguese desvios price series and the indexed-tariff formula were not found, so the PT versions of Experiments 1 and 2 cannot be finalised until REN's settlement pages or a client supplies them, while the Spanish version of Experiment 3 can run on OMIE and ESIOS immediately.

What each experiment does and does not buy commercially: Experiment 3 earns the first conversation with an optimiser because it speaks their language (capture ratio, €/MW/yr, aFRR share) and can be published openly as inbound marketing; Experiment 1 earns a conversation with a cooperative's Leiter Prognose but will be met with "show it on our balancing group", which only a data-sharing agreement answers; Experiment 2 mainly inoculates the founders against the residential temptation by reproducing HTW's 64 €/a finding on their own code.

## 7. Hardware decision: none is needed for the first paying customer

The brief asks whether proprietary hardware is necessary to land the first contract. It is not, for either wedge, and building it would be the costliest mistake available. The control-stack note ranks the lawful, repeatable paths by control availability, legal clarity, repeatability and value per integration hour: first, C&I and utility batteries through an existing direct marketer or EMS vendor (read-only shadow pilot first, then optimiser-as-a-service; a single 1 MW site at 150–260 k€/MW/yr is worth more than roughly a thousand German home batteries at 50–250 €/yr); second, V1G through OCPP or OEM telematics via a utility or CPO partner; third, residential batteries through a HEMS/integration partner on Modbus or local APIs, where the value is small in Germany and meaningful only in the Netherlands; fourth, heat pumps, whose value is dominated by tariff enrolment; last, V2G, which is OEM-locked hardware and bundles. No proprietary hardware is required for the first three.

The physical facts behind that ranking: local Modbus-TCP/SunSpec or local-API charge/discharge control exists for most 2020+ hybrid inverters sold in Germany (SMA, Fronius GEN24, Kostal Plenticore, Huawei SUN2000, SolarEdge hybrid, Sungrow SH, GoodWe, Growatt, Solax, Deye, Sigenergy, E3/DC, Fenecon, RCT, Victron, senec, Tesla via Fleet API), while Enphase, Varta, Solarwatt and older sonnen lines are read-only in the evcc registry; community templates control batteries behind 30–60 s watchdogs ([evcc device list](https://docs.evcc.io/de/meters)). HTW's 2026 measurements show that grid-charge arbitrage round-trip efficiency is 78% for the best system at 4 kW charge / 200 W discharge and that some systems return under 50% at 100 W discharge, so at 60% system efficiency the charge/discharge spread must exceed 14 ct/kWh; warranty throughput caps of 2.4–7.2 MWh per kWh and cycle caps of 3,120–10,000 mean two cycles a day exhausts a 3,120-cycle warranty in about 4.3 years ([HTW Stromspeicher-Inspektion 2026](https://solar.htw-berlin.de/wp-content/uploads/HTW-aquu-Stromspeicher-Inspektion-2026.pdf)). The regulated §14a path (SMGW plus BSI-certified CLS Steuerbox) is a DSO curtailment channel, not a market-dispatch channel: about eight boxes were certified by mid-2025, DSOs send "kaum Befehle", and most control runs as preventive time windows ([Innobu, 12 Jun 2026](https://www.innobu.com/de/artikel/steuerbox-14a-netzsteuerung-2026.html)); a market participant dispatches through the OEM cloud, a local API or an integration layer (gridX with 55+ OEMs and 500+ devices, OpenEMS/FEMS, evcc-class software) and inherits that layer's fail-safe ([gridX](https://www.gridx.ai/)). For C&I and utility batteries, balancing-market dispatch requires the asset to sit in a prequalified pool with 1–4 s telemetry and redundant links to the TSO, which only direct marketers, aggregators and large EMS vendors operate; a read-only shadow pilot needs only Modbus/EMS read access and public market data and involves no TSO interaction ([regelleistung.net PQ conditions](https://www.regelleistung.net/xspproxy/api/StaticFiles/Regelleistung/Infos_f%C3%BCr_Anbieter/Wie_werde_ich_Regelenergieanbieter_Pr%C3%A4qualifikation/Pr%C3%A4qualifikationsbedingungen_FCR_aFRR_mFRR/PQ-Bedingungen-Delta_2024_07_05.pdf)).

Unit-economics ranges per asset-year (gross, 2026 conditions, conservative / base / upside): 10 kWh German home battery on a dynamic tariff 50 / 120 / 250 €, plus ~100 € with Module 3; Dutch imbalance 700 / 1,200 / 2,000 € (measured €74–178 per month per 10 kW, falling); Dutch intraday via Tibber-type aggregator 900 / 1,100 / 1,500 € (company claims); Swedish FCR 90 / 200 / 360 €; German V1G EV 100 / 164 / 250 € market-only, 350 / 440 / 500 € with Modules 1+3; German heat pump 60 / 124 / 250 € market-only, 400 / 480 / 525 € with grid-fee modules; V2G per car 300 / 500 / 720 € under the availability-bonus structures; 1 MW/2 MWh German C&I battery 150 / 200 / 260 k€/MW/yr before optimiser fee, degradation and grid charges ([Neon, 27 Oct 2025](https://neon.energy/Neon-Dynamischer-Stromtarif.pdf); [Thuisbatterijnederland](https://thuisbatterijnederland.nl/onbalansmarkt-thuisbatterij/); [IAA Mobility](https://www.iaa-mobility.com/en/newsroom/news/future-technology/bmw-eon-officially-launch-bidirectional-v2g-offering-germany)). Net of a platform's take (CheckWatt's own calculator assumes 25% fees plus a 15% safety margin; Axle's take-rate is undisclosed), a third-party optimiser's share of a German residential asset is tens of euros a year, which is the arithmetic behind the B2B-only verdict ([CheckWatt](https://checkwatt.com/en/services/)). The cheapest compliant architecture if a hardware step is ever needed is the partner-led one: the partner's gateway (gridX gridBox, FEMS, an OEM cloud) carries the fail-safe and the BSI domain; the vendor never touches CLS. Open items the notes could not close: no OEM warranty text addressing third-party cycling was retrieved; no OEM VPP's contractual exclusivity (sonnen, senec, E3/DC, 1KOMMA5°, Sigenergy) was verified; NIS2 scope for aggregators and GDPR treatment of 15-minute meter data were not researched; and Iberian inverter mix and Mobi.E rules are entirely missing.

The precedent set is unambiguous about what each model becomes. Bespoke forecasting for utilities is a consulting business (price points of €2–25k/yr, 6–12-month pilots, buyers that build once they have a BI team, as EWR did with a self-reported seven-figure saving) ([ZfK, 9 Mar 2026](https://www.zfk.de/digitalisierung/wie-ewr-sich-mit-ki-strategisch-einen-vorsprung-verschafft)); forecasting-and-bidding SaaS to traders and optimisers is a niche software company that reaches 25–90 staff and €5–40m raised over five to eight years and exits to a Volue/Montel-type consolidator (Likron, PowerBot, Nnergix, EnAppSys); venture scale went to firms that took market risk or owned assets. The two paths to first revenue in the precedents were either founders from inside a trader with a capital partner (Flexpower traded within a month; enspired within six months) or software-only teams that took two to three years (suena, Whiffle, Jungle). This team is in the second group, which is why the recommendation is consulting-first on performance terms, with the licence as the month-12 goal and acquisition by an optimiser or market-data consolidator as the realistic upside. Contract hygiene the notes flag for counsel: vendor owns model and IP, client owns its data, time-boxed non-exclusivity, liability capped at fees with consequential loss excluded, audit rights and a kill switch offered proactively because REMIT Art. 5a puts algorithm responsibility on the client, and an Art. 28 processor agreement (Bitkom template) wherever household data is involved ([Bitkom](https://www.bitkom.org/Themen/Praxisleitfaeden-zur-Auftragsverarbeitung)).

## 10. Financing and the two-country company: grants first, incorporate after the grant decision

The cost base is small enough that the first question is sequencing, not fundraising. Numbeo puts single-person living costs excluding rent at €1,075/month in Munich, €760 in Lisbon and €719 in Porto, with one-bedroom rents outside the centre of €1,124, €1,063 and €852 respectively, so two founders split Munich/Lisbon need roughly €4,000/month of subsistence and about €48k for a year ([Numbeo Munich](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/in/Munich); [Numbeo Lisbon](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/in/Lisbon)). Adding ~$100/month of weather data, ~€50/month of IT liability insurance and a few hundred euros of cloud, the lean research scenario burns about €4.4–5.0k/month. The EXIST-Gründungsstipendium covers almost exactly that: €2,500/month per graduate (€3,000 for PhDs) for up to three people for twelve months plus up to €30,000 of material costs, open to all nationalities, requiring a German university host with a Gründungsnetzwerk, a degree or university employment no more than five years old, and — decisively — that the company "darf jedoch bei Projektbeginn noch nicht erfolgt sein" ([Förderdatenbank](https://www.foerderdatenbank.de/FDB/Content/DE/Foerderprogramm/Bund/BMWi/exist-gruendungsstipendium.html)). Bavaria's FLÜGGE (€2,500/month, once-a-year call, founders must belong to a Bavarian state university) has the same no-prior-incorporation rule and cannot be stacked with EXIST ([gesetze-bayern.de](https://www.gesetze-bayern.de/Content/Document/BayVV_7070_W_10427-0)). Whether an EXIST host accepts a team member resident in Portugal is unanswered in the Richtlinie and must be asked of PtJ and the host before applying.

EU instruments do not fit a late-2026 start: the EIC Accelerator's last 2026 cut-off is 4 November, Eurostars Call 11 closed 10 September 2026 with the next window expected in spring 2027, and none pays out in under six to nine months ([Hezelburcht](https://www.hezelburcht.com/en/news/eic-launches-2026-work-programme-an-overview-of-the-eic-instruments-for-2026/); [EUREKA](https://www.eurekanetwork.org/?p=1673)). Portuguese public instruments are narrow: the StartUP Voucher's 4th edition (€900/month, ≤29 years old, Norte/Centro/Alentejo, not employed) closed 30 January 2026, and Portugal Ventures (€50k–€1.5m) requires a Portuguese registered office ([ECO, 23 Jan 2026](https://eco.sapo.pt/2026/01/23/candidaturas-ao-startup-voucher-terminam-a-30-de-janeiro/); [Portugal Ventures](https://www.portugalventures.pt/en/about-us/investment-strategy-2/)).

Company geography, in order of fit for this plan: Option A, a German UG/GmbH founded during the EXIST grant with the Portuguese co-founder as employee, is cleanest for EXIST Phase II, HTGF and Bayern Kapital, but under Regulation 883/2004 an employee doing ≥25% of work in Portugal is insured there, so the GmbH must register as a Portuguese employer and pay 23.75% contributions, and a managing director working from a fixed place in Portugal risks a management permanent establishment under §12 AO — mitigate by making only the Munich founder Geschäftsführer ([steuerberaten.de](https://www.steuerberaten.de/blog/554/mitarbeiter-im-ausland-beschaeftigen-steuerliche-und-sozialversicherungsaspekte/); [PwC Portugal](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/portugal/individual/other-taxes)). Option C, a Portuguese Lda (Empresa na Hora €360; IRC 19%, 15% on the first €50k; startup-law stock options; Portugal Ventures and Faber eligibility; startup-reserved procurement), is cheaper and better for Wedge 2 but forfeits the German instruments and may unsettle Stadtwerke buyers ([registo.justica.gov.pt](https://registo.justica.gov.pt/Empresas/Empresa-na-Hora); [PwC Portugal corporate](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/portugal/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income)). A dual structure doubles compliance and is worth it only after a first round. The sequencing that respects the grant rules: do not incorporate until the EXIST or FLÜGGE decision; found the German entity during the grant if the Munich host route works; set up Portuguese payroll registration or an employer-of-record at that point; add the Lda when Portuguese public buyers or Portugal Ventures require it. Selling B2B software across EU borders needs no local establishment and VAT is reverse-charged; pilots of €5–30k sit far below the €432,000 sector threshold in Germany and inside Portugal's new <€75,000 direct-award limit ([Your Europe](https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/taxation/vat/cross-border-vat/index_en.htm)). Everything in this section marked by the researcher as [ADVISER] — PE risk, social security, REMIT PPAET status, insurance coverage of automated dispatch — needs a cross-border tax adviser and energy counsel before the first contract is signed.

## 11. The 90-day plan: four gates, one kill decision, four branches

The plan assumes two founders working full time from mid-October 2026, no entity until the grant decision, and a cash cost of roughly €15k over 90 days (subsistence plus data) if no grant has landed yet. Founder time splits roughly 60% technical (backtests, data pipelines) and 40% commercial (interviews, documents, events) for the Munich founder, and the reverse for the Portuguese founder.

The six- and twelve-month branches start from the day-90 memo. Branch (a), viable software-first wedge: a shadow pilot runs for one quarter on partner data, the performance-share contract converts at month 6, a second partner in the other wedge is signed by month 9, and by month 12 the team has two paying feeds, a working student, an advisor from a trading desk, and the scenario-2 evidence for a €300–500k pre-seed or a Eurostars consortium with a Portuguese utility partner; the metric is recurring revenue ≥ €10k/month by month 12. Branch (b), consultancy-first: diagnostics sell but no feed converts; the team runs three to five paid studies a year at €15–30k each (≈€60–120k revenue), stays bootstrapped, publishes, and uses the studies to find the one partner whose data shows a durable uplift; the metric is one converted feed within twelve months or an orderly stop. Branch (c), hardware-dependent opportunity: only if a partner's site shows that advice cannot be acted on without control (for example a C&I battery whose EMS cannot ingest a schedule) — then the FLÜGGE validation project or Eurostars funds a partner-led integration on the partner's gateway, never a proprietary device, and the team re-screens the small-C&I wedge (W5) with real site data. Branch (d), no-go or redirect: the kill criteria trip; the honest redirects are to join an optimiser's quant team (the precedents show that is where the two-person skill set is paid best), or to pivot the backtest tooling into a market-data product for the consolidators (Volue, Montel) who bought every pure model shop in the precedent list.

Two scheduled external events sit inside the window and should be used as gates in their own right: the Bundestag's second and third reading of the EEG 2027 draft (not scheduled as of 8 October 2026; planned entry into force 1 January 2027) decides whether hundreds of thousands of new small PV systems must find a direct marketer from 2027, which would strengthen the direct-marketer variant of Wedge 1 ([ema-energiewelt, 8 Oct 2026](https://ema-energiewelt.de/wissen/eeg-novelle-2027-kabinettsbeschluss-einspeiseverguetung-endet)); and the status of Portugal's 1,050 MVA storage auction decides whether a 2027 cohort of BESS owners exists for Wedge 2. Both should be checked in the first fifteen days and re-checked at day 90.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. The brief began from an anecdote about an individual who found a local inefficiency and sold model development to the organisations exposed to it; the notes show that in 2026 the exposed organisations are not the households, communities or installers the anecdote implies but the balance-responsible parties, optimisers and direct marketers one layer up, and that the "inefficiency" worth selling is not a mispriced hour but the gap between benchmark and realised dispatch under 15-minute settlement, compressing ancillary premiums and new regulatory products. That gap is measurable on public data within a month, which is the one structural advantage a two-person team with no network has over an incumbent: it can publish a decision-time backtest that an optimiser's quant cannot dismiss, and offer to be paid only on measured uplift. The Portuguese co-founder is not a market-size lever but a timing and access lever: Portugal's data, procurement code and post-blackout storage push let the team become the local-rule specialist that German optimisers arriving in Iberia will need, which turns Wedge 2 into a supplier relationship with Wedge 1's customers.

If I were starting this from Munich and Portugal with strong software/ML skills but limited industry access, I would first investigate the gap between benchmark and realised multi-market battery revenue in Germany and the imbalance cost of forecast error for Portuguese renewable-plus-storage owners, using Experiment 3 and Experiment 1 on SMARD, netztransparenz, OMIE and ESIOS data with a strict decision-time protocol, and publish the results within 30 days. I would approach Entrix and Kyon in Munich as a quant sub-supplier, Galp, Greenvolt and EDP's asset-management teams in Portugal through the co-founder, and Syneco's Leiter Prognose as the alternative, offering a free or sub-€5k technical evaluation under NDA and a performance-share term sheet. I would build an API-delivered 15-minute forecasting and bidding engine (aFRR capacity and energy first, intraday spreads second, co-location constraints third) with a REMIT-ready documentation pack and no hardware, no licence and no entity until the EXIST decision. And I would abandon it if, by day 90, the forecast-driven dispatcher is within five points of a rule-based one, no partner will share a month of realised data after ten conversations, or every buyer prices the work at the €2–25k a year that commodity forecast vendors already charge — in which case the honest redirect is to join an optimiser's quant desk or sell the backtest tooling to the market-data consolidators who have bought every pure model shop in the precedent list. The strong alternative, if the optimisers prove closed but the cooperatives prove open, is asymmetric-loss quarter-hourly load forecasting for Syneco, Südweststrom and Trianel on a shared-savings fee; it is a consulting business before it is a software one, and the plan should say so.
